## Two Things Called Morning Prayer

In Catholic usage the phrase means two different things, and mixing them up causes most of the confusion.

**Morning Prayer** with capitals is Lauds, one of the Hours of the Liturgy of the Hours, the Church's official daily prayer. The General Instruction on the Liturgy of the Hours quotes the Second Vatican Council: "Lauds as morning prayer and Vespers as evening prayer are the two hinges on which the daily Office turns" (GILH 37). Clergy are obliged to it. Lay Catholics are warmly encouraged and not bound.

**Catholic morning prayer** in ordinary speech means the short traditional prayers many Catholics say on waking: the Morning Offering, the Guardian Angel prayer, the Angelus at six. These take two minutes, require no book, and are what most people are searching for.

Both are below, prayers first.

## The Traditional Morning Prayers

### Sign of the Cross

> In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

### The Morning Offering

The version published by the USCCB, which credits it to Fr. François-Xavier Gautrelet in 1844 and to *Catholic Household Blessings and Prayers*:

> O Jesus, through the Immaculate Heart of Mary,
> I offer you my prayers, works, joys, and sufferings of this day
> for all the intentions of your Sacred Heart
> in union with the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass throughout the world,
> for the salvation of souls, the reparation of sins, the reunion of all Christians,
> and in particular for the intentions of the Holy Father this month.
> Amen.

This is the central prayer of a Catholic morning, and worth understanding rather than reciting. It hands over the whole day in advance, including the parts you have not lived yet and the parts that will go badly. The "sufferings of this day" clause is not asking for suffering. It is deciding, at 6am, what to do with whatever arrives.

### Prayer to Your Guardian Angel

> Angel of God, my guardian dear, to whom God's love commits me here, ever this day be at my side to light and guard, to rule and guide. Amen.

Most Catholics learn this one as children and keep it. "Ever this day" is the operative phrase in the morning.

### Come, Holy Spirit

> Come, O Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of your faithful and enkindle in them the fire of your love. Send forth your Spirit, and they shall be created. And you shall renew the face of the earth.
>
> Let us pray. O God, who has taught the hearts of the faithful by the light of the Holy Spirit, grant that by the gift of the same Spirit we may be always truly wise and ever rejoice in his consolation, through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### The Angelus

Traditionally prayed at six in the morning, noon and six in the evening.

> The angel of the Lord declared unto Mary.
> R. And she conceived of the Holy Spirit.
> (Hail Mary…)
>
> Behold the handmaid of the Lord.
> R. Be it done unto me according to thy word.
> (Hail Mary…)
>
> And the Word was made flesh.
> R. And dwelt among us.
> (Hail Mary…)
>
> Pray for us, O holy Mother of God.
> R. That we may be made worthy of the promises of Christ.
>
> Let us pray: Pour forth, we beseech thee, O Lord, thy grace into our hearts; that, we to whom the incarnation of Christ, thy Son, was made known by the message of an angel, may by his passion and cross, be brought to the glory of his resurrection, through the same Christ our Lord. Amen.

During Eastertide the Regina Caeli is prayed in its place.

### Prayer Before Meals

> Bless us O Lord, and these thy gifts, which we are about to receive, from thy bounty, through Christ, our Lord. Amen.

## A Two Minute Morning

If you want a sequence rather than a menu, this is the common one:

1. Sign of the Cross
2. Morning Offering
3. Guardian Angel prayer
4. One Our Father, one Hail Mary
5. Sign of the Cross

That is roughly ninety seconds and it is a complete Catholic morning prayer. Adding the Angelus takes it to three minutes. Nothing here requires a book, an app or a quiet room.

## Morning Prayer (Lauds) in the Liturgy of the Hours

If you want the Church's own morning prayer, here is what you are walking into.

The General Instruction describes Lauds as "designed to sanctify the morning" and quotes St. Basil the Great: "Matins consecrates to God the first movements of our minds and hearts; no other care should engage us before we have been moved with the thought of God" (GILH 38). The same paragraph notes that Lauds, "recited as the light of a new day dawns, recalls the resurrection of the Lord Jesus, the true light."

The structure, per GILH 41 to 53:

| Part | What happens |
|---|---|
| Introductory verse | "O God, come to our aid: O Lord, make haste to help us," then the Glory be with Alleluia (omitted in Lent) |
| Hymn | Chosen to fit the Hour or feast |
| Psalmody | One morning psalm, an Old Testament canticle, and a second psalm of praise |
| Reading | A short reading, or a longer one in celebrations with people |
| Responsory | A short response to the reading, optional |
| Benedictus | The Canticle of Zechariah with its antiphon, recited solemnly |
| Intercessions | Which "consecrate the day and its work to God" |
| Our Father | Said by all |
| Concluding prayer | From the psalter on ferial days, from the Proper otherwise |

The Benedictus is Luke 1:68-79, and its closing lines are why it belongs to the morning (NABRE):

> "…because of the tender mercy of our God by which the daybreak from on high will visit us to shine on those who sit in darkness and death's shadow, to guide our feet into the path of peace."

Praying Lauds takes ten to fifteen minutes. You need a breviary or an app that assembles the day for you; several are compared on our [catholic prayer app](/resources/catholic-prayer-app) page, with Universalis and Divine Office the two most straightforward starting points.

## Scripture for the Morning

All quotations from the New American Bible, Revised Edition (NABRE), the translation approved for use in the United States.

- **Psalm 5:4** "in the morning you will hear my voice; in the morning I will plead before you and wait."
- **Psalm 143:8** "In the morning let me hear of your mercy, for in you I trust. Show me the path I should walk, for I entrust my life to you."
- **Lamentations 3:22-23** "The Lord's acts of mercy are not exhausted, his compassion is not spent; They are renewed each morning."
- **Mark 1:35** "Rising very early before dawn, he left and went off to a deserted place, where he prayed."

That last one is the argument for the whole practice. Whatever Jesus was doing before dawn, he was doing it before anything else.

## The Phone Problem

The obstacle to Catholic morning prayer is almost never theological. It is that the phone is on the nightstand and gets picked up before your feet touch the floor. By the time you have read three notifications, the first movements of your mind and heart have gone somewhere, and it was not to God.

Two fixes, in order of effectiveness.

Charge the phone in another room. This costs nothing, works immediately, and no app will ever beat it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the Catholic morning prayer?**
Most often the Morning Offering: "O Jesus, through the Immaculate Heart of Mary, I offer you my prayers, works, joys, and sufferings of this day…" It is usually said with the Sign of the Cross and the Guardian Angel prayer, and takes under two minutes.

**What time should I say the Angelus?**
Six in the morning, noon and six in the evening, by long custom. It is not an obligation and nothing goes wrong if you pray it at seven. During Eastertide the Regina Caeli replaces it.

**Do lay Catholics have to pray the Liturgy of the Hours?**
No. The obligation to recite the whole sequence of Hours falls on bishops, priests and deacons aspiring to the priesthood (GILH 29). The General Instruction recommends Lauds and Vespers to "the individual faithful who are not able to participate in a common celebration" (GILH 40), which is an encouragement, not a requirement.

**What is the difference between Lauds and Matins?**
Lauds is Morning Prayer. Matins is the older name for the night office, now called the Office of Readings, which can be prayed at any hour of the day. Some older texts, including the St. Basil quotation in GILH 38, use "Matins" where we would now say morning prayer, which is a real source of confusion when reading older spiritual writers.

**Can I pray the Morning Offering later in the day?**
Yes, and it is better than not praying it. The prayer offers the day; a day offered at 11am is still a day offered. The reason to do it first is practical, not legal.

## The Verdict

Learn the Morning Offering by heart and say it before you touch the phone. That single habit does more than any app, any plan and any resolution about the Liturgy of the Hours you are not going to keep. Once it is automatic, and only then, look at Lauds. Starting with the full Office is how most people end up praying nothing at all by March.
