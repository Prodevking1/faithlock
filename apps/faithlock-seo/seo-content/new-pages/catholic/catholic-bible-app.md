## What Makes a Bible App Catholic

The line is not the app's name. It is the canon.

The Catholic canon has 73 books, 46 in the Old Testament and 27 in the New. The Catechism sets the list out at paragraph 120 and names, among the Old Testament books, "Tobit, Judith, Esther, 1 and 2 Maccabees ... the Wisdom of Solomon, Sirach (Ecclesiasticus) ... Baruch." Protestant Bibles print 66 books and omit those seven, along with the Greek portions of Esther and Daniel. Catholics call the seven deuterocanonical; most Protestant Bibles either omit them or group them in a separate apocrypha section.

So the test for a catholic bible app is simple. Open the table of contents and look for Sirach. If it is not there, the app is carrying a Protestant canon regardless of what the icon says.

The second thing to check is the translation. Common Catholic English translations include:

- **NABRE** (New American Bible, Revised Edition), approved for the United States and the basis of the American lectionary
- **RSV-CE** and **RSV-2CE** (Revised Standard Version, Catholic Edition and Second Catholic Edition), preferred by many for study
- **Douay-Rheims**, the traditional English translation from the Vulgate
- **Jerusalem Bible**, used in liturgical texts outside the United States

## A Note on FaithLock and the Catholic Canon

We should be straight about this, because the honest answer is useful to you.

[FaithLock](/) is an iOS app that blocks distracting apps using Apple's Family Controls and shows a Bible verse with a short comprehension question when you try to open one. Its built in library is the complete Berean Standard Bible, 31,000+ verses. The BSB is a Protestant translation of the 66 book canon. It does not contain Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch, 1 or 2 Maccabees, or the Greek portions of Esther and Daniel, and there is no Catholic edition of the app.

That makes it a poor choice as your catholic bible app and a reasonable one as the thing that stops Instagram from eating the twenty minutes you meant to spend in Ascension or Laudate. Those are different jobs. If you want a verse in front of you when you reach for the wrong app, and a Catholic canon in your reading app, run both.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does the Catholic Bible have 73 books?**
Yes. The Catechism of the Catholic Church, paragraph 120, states that the canon "includes 46 books for the Old Testament (45 if we count Jeremiah and Lamentations as one) and 27 for the New," and lists Tobit, Judith, 1 and 2 Maccabees, Wisdom and Sirach among them. That is 73.

**What is the best free Catholic Bible app?**
Laudate, if you want two Catholic translations plus the Catechism at no download cost. Ascension, if you want a better reading experience and are willing to live inside a paid content ecosystem for the extras; its listing states the full Bible and Catechism text are available to all users.

**Which Bible translation do Catholics use in the United States?**
The NABRE is the approved translation in the US and the basis of the lectionary. The RSV-CE and RSV-2CE are widely used for study, and the Douay-Rheims remains in use among Catholics who prefer the traditional English text.

**Does the YouVersion Bible app have the Catholic books?**
Its App Store listing does not state which canons or Catholic translations are included, so we did not verify it and will not claim either way. If you use it, check the translation picker for a Catholic edition and look for Sirach in the contents before relying on it.

**What are the deuterocanonical books?**
Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch, and 1 and 2 Maccabees, plus additional Greek sections of Esther and Daniel. Catholics receive them as Scripture; most Protestant Bibles omit them or set them apart as apocrypha.
