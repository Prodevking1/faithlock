## What a Novena Actually Is

A novena is nine consecutive days of prayer for a particular intention. The 1913 Catholic Encyclopedia, hosted by Catholic Answers, defines it as "a nine days' private or public devotion in the Catholic Church to obtain special graces," from the Latin *novem*, nine.

Two honest notes about its status, since apps rarely mention either.

First, the same entry states that the novena "is permitted and even recommended by ecclesiastical authority, but still has no proper and fully set place in the liturgy of the Church." A novena is a devotion, not a liturgical act. Missing a day does not invalidate a sacrament, because no sacrament is involved.

Second, on origins: Catholics commonly trace the practice to the nine days between the Ascension and Pentecost, when Acts records that the apostles "devoted themselves with one accord to prayer, together with some women, and Mary the mother of Jesus, and his brothers" (Acts 1:14, NABRE). That reading follows from Acts 1:3, where the risen Christ appears "during forty days," with Pentecost falling on the fiftieth. The Catholic Encyclopedia entry is more cautious and traces the nine day form to Greek and Roman mourning customs that the Church later absorbed and repurposed. Both accounts circulate. We are not going to resolve a historical question the encyclopedists left open.

What matters practically is the shape: nine days, one intention, one prayer repeated. Which is where apps earn their keep, because the failure mode of a novena is never devotion. It is forgetting day five.

## The Best Catholic Novena App: 5 Picks for 2026

### 1. Intercede

**Best for**: anyone who has started a novena and lost it on day four

Intercede, formerly the Pray Catholic Novenas app, does novenas and nothing else. Its listing describes itself as "uniquely focused on novenas, which are multi-day prayers, some extending up to nine months," and says it "focuses exclusively on traditional Catholic devotions, distinguishing it from other apps that cater to a broader Christian audience." The library is organized by intention (healing, discernment, employment) and runs from the Sacred Heart novena to St. Jude for impossible cases. Each novena carries a saint biography. You can add a personal intention that stays visible while you pray, invite family into a shared novena, and review a history of what you have completed.

**What stands out**: the reminders and the completion history. Together they turn a nine day commitment into something you can actually finish, which no amount of beautiful typography does.

**Where it falls short**: it is one tool. No Mass readings, no Bible, no Office. And at 4.95 stars across 35,638 ratings it is the highest rated app on this page, which is worth knowing but also worth discounting slightly: devotional apps attract generous raters.

## Finishing the Nine Days

The apps above will remind you. Whether the reminder survives contact with your lock screen is a different question, and it usually does not: the notification arrives, you unlock the phone, and forty minutes later you are somewhere else entirely.

If that is your pattern, the fix is not a better novena app. It is removing the thing that intercepts you. [FaithLock](/) is an iOS app that blocks chosen apps on a schedule using Apple's Family Controls, so the window you set aside for the novena stays a window. Note what it is not: its Bible library is the Berean Standard Bible, a Protestant translation without the deuterocanonical books, and it carries no novena texts at all. Use it next to a catholic novena app, not instead of one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best Catholic novena app?**
Intercede, if novenas are the reason you are installing something. It is the only app on this list built around the nine day structure, with reminders, a personal intention that stays attached to the novena, shared novenas and a completion history.

**Is there a free Catholic novena app?**
Yes. The Catholic Novena App, Catholic Prayers: Official and Laudate were all free to download as of 2026-08-16, and Intercede's core app is free with optional patron tiers.

**What happens if I miss a day of a novena?**
Nothing is invalidated. A novena is a devotion rather than a liturgical or sacramental act, and the Catholic Encyclopedia entry notes it "has no proper and fully set place in the liturgy of the Church." Common practice is simply to continue from where you left off, or to begin again if the continuity mattered to you. Ask your parish priest if you want guidance for a particular devotion, since some indulgenced novenas attach their own conditions.

**How many days is a novena?**
Nine, from the Latin *novem*. Some devotions extend the pattern: the 54 Day Rosary Novena is six novenas run back to back, twenty seven days of petition and twenty seven of thanksgiving.

**Can I pray a novena for someone else?**
Yes, and it is one of the most common uses. Intercede's shared novena feature exists specifically so a family can pray the same nine days for the same intention.
