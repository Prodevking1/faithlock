## Key Takeaways

- **Best overall**: iRosary (beads that move under your finger, audio that varies each time, no ads, no tracking)
- **Best guided audio**: Ascension: Catholic Bible (the Rosary with Fr. Mike Schmitz, the Sisters of Life and others, in English or Latin, with chant)
- **Best scriptural Rosary**: Amen (standard, meditative and scriptural formats)
- **Best free with zero strings**: Rosary + Divine Mercy Chaplet (auto selects the day's mysteries; the listing says it will "ALWAYS remain" free)
- **Best for other chaplets too**: Laudate (Latin Rosary, Seven Sorrows, Franciscan Crown, Servite Rosary and more)

## How We Picked These Apps

Ratings, publishers, prices, versions and last update dates come from the Apple App Store listing API, checked 2026-08-16. Feature descriptions come from each app's own listing.

Four criteria decided the order. Does the app rotate the mysteries by day automatically, or make you pick every time? Is there audio, and is it bearable for twenty minutes? Does it track your place well enough that you can pray with your eyes half closed? And is it still maintained, because several highly rated rosary apps have not shipped an update in years.

We did not rank by star rating alone. This category has apps at 4.8 stars that stopped being updated in 2018.

If you are new to the devotion itself, read [how to pray the rosary](/resources/how-to-pray-the-rosary) first. An app will keep your place; it will not teach you the shape of the prayer.

## The Best Rosary App: 6 Picks for 2026

### 1. iRosary

**Best for**: praying with your hands, on a phone

iRosary's listing describes a rosary that "moves like a physical rosary" and advances by dragging the beads with your finger, placeable on either side of the screen. Its audio feature mixes multiple recordings of each prayer so a fresh track is generated every time, meaning the same voice arrives with a different cadence prayer to prayer rather than looping identically. Studio recorded male and female voices can play alone or alternate parts, with a speed control. It works with AirPods, CarPlay and the Lock Screen audio controls. Each mystery carries curated high resolution paintings, with El Greco and Murillo named in the listing. The listing states plainly: no ads within the app, and no tracking of your usage.

**What stands out**: the varying audio. Recorded rosary apps become wallpaper after a week because your ear learns the loop. Mixing the recordings is a small idea that solves the largest problem in the category.

**Where it falls short**: the best parts (always-new audio, the extra paintings, the 180,000+ bead designs) sit behind an Ultra upgrade. The free tier is a working rosary, not the full experience.

### 2. Ascension: Catholic Bible

**Best for**: praying along with a voice you already trust

Ascension's listing offers recordings of the Joyful, Luminous, Sorrowful and Glorious mysteries led by Fr. Mike Schmitz, Fr. Mark-Mary Ames, Jeff Cavins, the Sisters of Life and Paul Rose, in English or Latin, with the option to chant the prayers. The Rosary sits inside a larger app carrying the Great Adventure Catholic Bible, the full Catechism, the daily Mass readings and the Rosary in a Year podcast.

**What stands out**: the range of guides. Praying the Sorrowful Mysteries with the Sisters of Life is a genuinely different experience from praying them with Fr. Mike, and having both is worth more than a fifth bead texture.

**Where it falls short**: it is a Bible app that includes a rosary, so you navigate past a lot of other content. Subscription required for parts of the library beyond the Bible, Catechism and daily readings.

### 3. Amen

**Best for**: the scriptural Rosary

Amen, from the Augustine Institute, offers the Rosary in standard, meditative and scriptural formats, with optional background music. In the scriptural form a line of Scripture accompanies each Hail Mary, which keeps the mystery in front of your mind through all ten beads instead of only at the announcement.

**What stands out**: it is the cleanest fix for the single most common complaint about the Rosary, which is that attention drifts somewhere around the fourth Hail Mary.

**Where it falls short**: fewer voices and fewer visual options than Ascension or iRosary, and no Latin per its listing.

## The Problem No Rosary App Solves

Every app here keeps your place. None of them keeps your phone from interrupting you, and that is the real reason a Rosary started at 9pm finishes at 9:40 with two decades missing.

Two fixes. The low tech one is beads plus airplane mode, and it works better than anything you can install. The other is blocking the apps that intercept you. [FaithLock](/) is an iOS app that blocks chosen apps on a schedule using Apple's Family Controls, and shows a Bible verse with a short question when you try to open one anyway. It is not a rosary app and has no rosary content. Its verse library is the Berean Standard Bible, a Protestant translation that omits the deuterocanonical books, so it is not a Catholic resource either. It guards the twenty minutes. What you do with them is between you and the beads.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best free rosary app?**
iRosary for the experience, since its free tier is a fully working rosary. Rosary + Divine Mercy Chaplet if you want something that opens, picks today's mysteries and starts, with no upgrade path at all. Both were free to download as of 2026-08-16.

**Is there a rosary app that tells you what to say?**
Yes. Ascension, Amen and Hallow all provide guided audio that speaks the prayers so you can follow along. Ascension offers several different guides and both English and Latin. If you would rather read than listen, iRosary displays the prayer text as you advance the beads.

**Which rosary app changes the mysteries automatically?**
Rosary + Divine Mercy Chaplet states in its listing that it "automatically selects the mysteries by day." Most of the others let you pick, which matters less than it sounds since the assignment is easy to memorize: Monday and Saturday Joyful, Tuesday and Friday Sorrowful, Wednesday and Sunday Glorious, Thursday Luminous.

**Do rosary apps work offline?**
It varies, and most listings do not say. Assume guided audio needs a connection unless the app states otherwise. Text based apps such as iRosary and Laudate are the safer bet for a plane or a basement chapel.

**Is it acceptable to pray the Rosary on a phone?**
The beads are a counting aid, not a requirement, and the Church has never taught otherwise. The practical objection is not theological: a phone brings notifications, and notifications end prayer. If you pray on a phone, turn on Do Not Disturb first.

## The Verdict

Buy actual beads. Then, for the days you do not have them, install iRosary: it is the only app here that respects the tactile part of the devotion, and its varying audio is the one feature in this category that solves a real problem rather than adding a texture. If you want a voice to carry you, Ascension has the deepest bench of guides and it costs nothing to try.
