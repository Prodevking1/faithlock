## Quick Verdict
- **Choose Holy Focus if**: You want beautiful, curated prayers replacing your distraction moments
- **Choose One Sec if**: You want a proven, secular mindfulness pause that works on both iOS and Android

## Holy Focus in 30 Seconds

Holy Focus intercepts blocked apps with handpicked prayers, Bible verses, and custom faith-based icons. Over 300 prayer-and-verse combinations make each interruption feel like a devotional card. The visual design is among the best in faith-based apps. iOS-only, freemium at ~$6.49/month, rated 4.8 stars.

## One Sec in 30 Seconds

One Sec places a breathing exercise between you and your distracting apps. You take a conscious breath, the app asks if you still want to open the app, and that pause alone reduces app-opens by 57% according to One Sec's published data. Simple, backed by behavioral psychology, and available on iOS and Android. Freemium with premium at ~$39.99/year.

## Key Differences

### Content vs Breath
Holy Focus fills the interruption with spiritual content — prayers and verses designed to redirect your heart. One Sec fills it with a physical action — a conscious breath designed to interrupt autopilot behavior. One is about receiving something (God's Word). The other is about doing something (breathing intentionally). Both work, but they work on different people.

### Blocking Strength
Holy Focus replaces the blocked app with prayer content — you can't reach the app until you've seen the prayer. One Sec pauses you, then asks "do you still want to open this?" You can say yes and proceed. Holy Focus creates a harder block. One Sec creates a softer nudge. The 57% reduction in app-opens suggests the nudge is plenty effective for most people.

### Platform Availability
One Sec works on Android. Holy Focus doesn't. For Android users, One Sec is the option.

### Data and Proof
One Sec publishes clear metrics: 57% reduction in app-opens, hours saved per week, reduction trends over time. Holy Focus doesn't quantify results the same way. If seeing "you opened Instagram 57% less this week" motivates you, One Sec provides that feedback loop.

## Which Should You Choose?

**You want prayer in your screen time interruptions**: Holy Focus. One Sec has no spiritual content.

**You want the statistically proven approach**: One Sec. The 57% reduction figure is backed by their user data and consistent with behavioral psychology research.

**You're on Android**: One Sec.

**You want the most beautiful intervention experience**: Holy Focus. Each prayer screen is designed like a devotional card.

**You want harder blocking**: Holy Focus. One Sec lets you choose to proceed.

**You want to track your progress with numbers**: One Sec. Its metrics dashboard shows your improvement over time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does One Sec's breathing exercise really change behavior?**
One Sec reports a 57% reduction in distracting app-opens. The mechanism — forcing a conscious pause before an automatic action — aligns with established research on habit interruption. User reviews broadly support these claims, though individual results vary.

**Can I customize what Holy Focus shows me?**
You can set some content preferences, but the specific prayer-verse combination for each interruption is curated by the Holy Focus team. This curation is part of the experience — they've selected prayers that work well in those brief moments.

**Which app works better for social media specifically?**
Both work well. Holy Focus's hard blocking prevents you from reaching the social media app at all until you've engaged with prayer. One Sec's pause is specifically effective for social media because the breathing exercise breaks the reflexive muscle-memory tap.

**Is there a faith-based app with the data tracking of One Sec?**
Not at One Sec's level. FaithLock tracks streaks and engagement, but no faith-based blocker currently matches the analytics depth of secular tools like One Sec or Opal.

**Can I use Holy Focus for prayer time beyond app blocking?**
Some users open Holy Focus deliberately for prayer content, separate from the blocking function. The 300+ prayer library has standalone devotional value.
