Deciding between Canopy vs Covenant Eyes usually comes down to one question: do you want the content filtered automatically, or do you want a trusted person reviewing a report of your activity?

## Quick Verdict
- **Choose Canopy if**: You want content actually filtered in real time across the most devices, and you can tolerate occasional overblocking and a slower connection
- **Choose Covenant Eyes if**: You specifically want the accountability partner model, where a trusted person sees a report of your activity, plus structured recovery programs

## Canopy vs Covenant Eyes at a Glance

If you searched for either "canopy vs covenant eyes" or "covenant eyes vs canopy," you're looking at the same two apps from different angles, and the comparison below covers both directions. Neither app blocks apps the way FaithLock does; both are built around adult content safety, just through different mechanisms.

## Canopy in 30 Seconds

Canopy, made by Netspark Inc., runs on iOS, Android, Windows, macOS, and ChromeOS. It installs a VPN profile on the device (visible under Settings > VPN & Device Management on iOS) and uses AI to scan traffic in real time, blurring explicit images rather than blocking a whole site outright. It also blocks known third-party VPNs to reduce one common workaround. Pricing is subscription-only across Essential, Plus, and Premium tiers based on device count, billed annually, with a one-week free trial followed by a 30-day money-back guarantee.

## Covenant Eyes in 30 Seconds

Covenant Eyes, now marketed under the Victory by Covenant Eyes name, runs on iOS, Android, Windows, and macOS. Its core mechanism is reporting, not blocking: on Android, Windows, and macOS, it takes regular AI-analyzed screenshots and sends a report to an accountability partner you choose. On iOS, it adds a filtering VPN that blocks adult content at the network level, plus a Safari extension that captures screenshots, but only inside Safari, not in other browsers or apps. It's subscription-only, with a 30-day trial and money-back guarantee, and one subscription covers up to 10 users and unlimited devices in a household.

## Key Differences

### Filtering vs Reporting
This is the real dividing line. Canopy tries to stop explicit content before it renders, using a VPN and AI image analysis. Covenant Eyes, outside its narrower iOS VPN filter, mostly documents what happened and sends that report to someone else afterward. If you want the content itself intercepted, Canopy is doing more of that work technically. If you want a human in the loop reviewing your activity, that's the entire point of Covenant Eyes.

**Winner: Canopy**, for anyone who wants the block to happen at the content level rather than relying on someone else's review after the fact.

### Bypass Resistance
Neither app is airtight, and both concede that honestly in their own support material or independent reviews. Canopy's VPN and third-party-VPN blocking make it closer to a genuine technical barrier, but reviewers note overblocking of harmless content and at least one case of a user finding a workaround via image search after a few weeks. Covenant Eyes is weaker specifically on iOS: it has no Screen Time integration, the app can simply be deleted, and screenshot capture only works inside Safari, leaving every other browser and app unmonitored. An independent tester downloaded a dating app and received suggestive photos by message without triggering any alert.

**Winner: Canopy**, marginally, since its VPN-level approach covers more of the device than Covenant Eyes' Safari-only screenshot capture on iOS.

### Accountability Model and Recovery Support
Covenant Eyes' real strength isn't technical, it's relational. The accountability partner report, plus the associated Strive (for men) and Arise (for women) recovery programs, gives it a structure built around ongoing recovery rather than a single content filter. Canopy has no equivalent accountability-partner or recovery-program layer; it's a filtering tool, full stop.

**Winner: Covenant Eyes**, for anyone whose actual need is an accountability relationship and recovery support, not just a filter.

## Honest Concessions

Canopy users report real friction: a 3.8 out of 5 rating on the App Store across roughly 1,300 reviews cites overblocking of non-explicit content, heavy battery and data use, and conflicts with banking or streaming apps. On Trustpilot, ratings run closer to 2.2 to 2.5 out of 5, with complaints about a slow VPN connection and refunds not honored.

Covenant Eyes users report cancellation difficulties and continued billing after requesting the service be stopped, according to Trustpilot reviews, and its iOS coverage gap (Safari-only screenshots, no Screen Time integration) is a real limitation for anyone assuming it monitors the whole device.

## Which Should You Choose?

**You want the closest thing to an actual content block**: Canopy. Its VPN-based filtering intercepts more than Covenant Eyes' Safari-only screenshot approach.

**You want a specific person reviewing your activity, not just a filter**: Covenant Eyes. That's its entire design, and the Strive/Arise programs add structure a filter alone doesn't.

**You're mostly on iOS**: Be aware both have documented iOS-specific weaknesses. Canopy's VPN covers the whole device; Covenant Eyes' screenshot capture on iOS is limited to Safari.

**Cost matters more than accountability structure**: Compare current tier pricing directly on canopy.us and covenanteyes.com, since both are subscription-only with tiers that change.

However you land on Canopy vs Covenant Eyes, remember both are content-safety tools for adult material specifically, not general-purpose app blockers.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Canopy or Covenant Eyes better for a teenager?**
Canopy is built more explicitly around content filtering across a family's devices, which fits a parental use case more directly. Covenant Eyes is generally used by adults with a chosen accountability partner, and its recovery programs (Strive, Arise) are adult-oriented.

**Does Covenant Eyes actually block anything on iPhone?**
Partially. It runs a filtering VPN that blocks adult content at the network level on iOS, plus a Safari extension for screenshots. Outside Safari, on other browsers and apps, there's no screenshot monitoring, which is a real gap compared to how the service works on Android, Windows, or macOS.

**Can Canopy be bypassed?**
Not easily, since it blocks known third-party VPNs, but it isn't airtight. At least one App Store reviewer reported finding a workaround through image search after using it for a few weeks.

**Do I need both apps?**
Some households run a content filter like Canopy alongside a separate accountability-partner tool, but most people pick one based on whether they want a technical filter or a relational accountability structure, not both at once.

**Canopy vs Covenant Eyes: which one actually blocks content instead of just reporting it?**
Canopy is closer to a real block, since its VPN filters and blurs content before it fully renders. Covenant Eyes mostly documents what happened, outside its narrower iOS VPN filter, and sends that report to your accountability partner afterward.
