## What Is Bible Mode?

Bible Mode is a faith-based app blocker developed by Friday Labs LLC that takes a radically different approach from every other app in this category. Instead of showing you a verse on screen and asking you to read it, Bible Mode wants you to pick up your actual, physical Bible.

Here's the pitch: you block distracting apps, and when you try to open one, you scan a page from your real Bible with your phone camera. The app uses text recognition to confirm you've read it, then unlocks your apps.

It sounds gimmicky until you try it. There's something about physically reaching for your Bible — opening it, finding a passage, holding your phone over the page — that changes the dynamic completely. You're not just tapping through a screen. You're engaging with Scripture the way Christians have for centuries, and your phone becomes the reason you do it.

---

## How It Works

1. **Download and set up** Bible Mode from the App Store
2. **Select which apps** you want to block (social media, games, news — whatever pulls you in)
3. When you try to open a blocked app, Bible Mode intercepts it
4. You're given two options: **scan your physical Bible** or **use the in-app verse reflection**
5. If you choose scanning, point your camera at an open Bible page — the app reads the text and confirms it
6. Once confirmed, your blocked apps unlock for a set period
7. The in-app option shows devotionals, prayers, and verse reflections (KJV and ESV available)

The scanning process takes about 10-15 seconds in good lighting. You don't need to read a specific passage — any page of Scripture works. The app recognizes Bible text and distinguishes it from other books.

## Features Breakdown

**Physical Bible Scanning**
This is the headline feature and it genuinely works well. The OCR (optical character recognition) is solid with decent lighting. It recognizes both KJV and ESV text reliably. The experience of physically opening your Bible every time you want to check Instagram creates a powerful habit loop — and honestly, most of the time you end up reading more than the minimum.

**In-App Devotionals**
When you're at work or somewhere without your Bible, the app has a fallback: verse reflections, short devotionals, and prayer affirmations. These are thoughtfully curated, not just random verses. The content rotates daily.

**Prayer Affirmations**
Bible Mode includes faith-based affirmations tied to Scripture. These appear alongside the unlock experience and are meant to ground you before you re-enter the app world.

**App Blocking**
The blocking itself uses Apple's Screen Time API (Family Controls framework), which means it's system-level. You can't just force-quit Bible Mode to get around it. You select which apps to block during setup and can adjust the list anytime.

**Translation Support**
Currently supports KJV and ESV for the scanning feature. The in-app content draws from both translations. If you primarily read NIV or NASB, the scanning should still work (it's recognizing Bible text patterns, not specific translations), but the in-app content is limited to those two.

## Who Should Look Elsewhere?

**Android users.** Bible Mode is iOS-only with no announced Android plans.

**People who want scheduling or analytics.** If you want to block apps only during certain hours (morning devotion, work hours, bedtime) or track your screen time trends, Bible Mode doesn't offer that. It's an always-on blocker, not a time management tool.

**Those who prefer active engagement over passive reading.** Bible Mode confirms you scanned a page, but it doesn't quiz you or ask you to reflect on what you read. If you want something that makes sure you're actually absorbing Scripture, apps with quiz-based unlocking might serve you better.

**People without a physical Bible nearby.** If you're always on the go and don't carry your Bible, you'll default to the in-app devotionals most of the time — which are fine, but you're missing the app's strongest feature.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does the scanning work with any Bible translation?**
The OCR technology recognizes Bible text patterns broadly, so most English translations should work for scanning. The in-app devotional content is limited to KJV and ESV, but the scanning feature itself is more flexible. Users have reported success with NIV, NASB, and NLT physical Bibles.

**Can I use a Bible app on another device instead of a physical Bible?**
No. Bible Mode specifically requires a physical Bible. The camera needs to see printed text on a page. This is intentional — the whole point is getting you off screens and into a physical book.

**What happens if I'm somewhere without my Bible?**
The app has an in-app fallback with devotionals, verse reflections, and prayer affirmations. You won't be permanently locked out of your apps just because you left your Bible at home.

**Does it work in low light?**
It can struggle. The text recognition needs decent lighting to read the page clearly. A bedside lamp works fine, but trying to scan in a dark room will frustrate you. The developers have improved this over updates, but it's still the app's biggest technical limitation.

**Can my kids bypass the blocker?**
Bible Mode uses Apple's Screen Time API at the system level, which makes it harder to bypass than most third-party blockers. That said, it's designed as a self-accountability tool, not parental control software. A tech-savvy teenager could find workarounds.

**Is the $59.99 lifetime purchase worth it?**
If you plan to use the app for more than 6-9 months, yes. Compare it to Holy Focus at ~$6.49/month ($78/year) or other subscriptions. The lifetime price pays for itself quickly. The risk is that you try it and don't stick with it — so start with the free tier first.

**Does Bible Mode drain battery?**
No. It uses Apple's native Screen Time API, which runs at the system level with minimal battery impact. Users consistently report negligible battery drain — well under 1% per day.

**Can I choose which apps to block?**
Yes. During setup, you select specific apps to block. You can modify this list anytime. Most users block social media (Instagram, TikTok, X/Twitter), games, and news apps.
