## Quick Verdict

Brick trades a software toggle for a physical object. Tap the little NFC brick to lock your apps, tap it again to get them back. That physical ritual is a genuinely strong habit breaker for a lot of people, but at least one App Store reviewer reports that Strict Mode, the setting meant to stop you from cheating, can be defeated in one tap through iOS Screen Time settings. This Brick app review walks through what actually holds up and what doesn't.

## Key Takeaways

- Brick blocks apps until you physically **tap an NFC hardware device**, not a code or a timer
- The companion app is **free**, but you need to buy the physical Brick hardware separately
- **Strict Mode** is meant to prevent tampering, though a named App Store reviewer reports it can be bypassed in one step via iOS Screen Time settings
- Losing your Brick isn't fatal: you get **five Emergency Unbrick uses** per account before you need to contact support
- Available on **iOS and Android**, though website blocking isn't supported on Android yet
- Best for people who respond to a **physical, tactile ritual**, not a screen based one

## What Is Brick?

Brick is made by Brick LLC, and it flips the usual screen time app formula. Instead of asking you to trust yourself to tap "start focus session" on a screen, you physically tap your phone against a small NFC hardware device, the actual Brick, to lock a set of apps, and tap it again to unlock them.

The idea is that friction works better when it's real. A software toggle lives on the same device you're trying to put down. A physical Brick sitting on your desk or hanging on your keys does not. You choose a Mode (Deep Work, Family Time, Sleep, Study, Social Time, and more), tap the Brick, and your selected apps disappear from reach until you tap it again.

This Brick app review looks at whether that physical ritual holds up under real use, and where it doesn't.

## Is Brick Free?

**The Brick companion app is free with no subscription, but using it requires buying the physical Brick hardware, a one time purchase.** The exact hardware price could not be independently verified with a dated source, so check Brick's official site for the current price before ordering.

There's no recurring subscription fee for the software itself. Once you own a Brick, locking and unlocking apps costs nothing extra.

## How It Works

1. **Download** the free Brick app on iOS or Android
2. **Order and receive** the physical NFC Brick device
3. **Choose a Mode**: Deep Work, Family Time, Sleep, Study, Social Time, or a custom setup
4. **Select which apps** to block for that Mode
5. **Tap your phone to the Brick** to start the blocking session
6. Apps stay locked until you **tap the Brick again**
7. If you lose the Brick, you get **five Emergency Unbrick uses** per account before needing a support request, typically processed in about 48 hours

The tap itself takes a couple of seconds. Getting up and walking to wherever you left the Brick is the actual point.

## Features Breakdown

**Physical NFC Tap**
The core mechanic. No PIN, no in app timer you can simply close out of, no toggle. You need the Brick in physical contact with your phone to start or stop blocking.

**Custom Modes**
Deep Work, Family Time, Sleep, Study, Social Time, and other presets let you tailor which apps get blocked in which context, rather than a single all or nothing block.

**Strict Mode**
An optional setting meant to prevent app deletion or changing your block list mid session. It's a real feature, but see the weaknesses below: it isn't foolproof.

**One Time Purchase**
The companion app has no subscription. You pay once for the hardware and use the app for free after that.

**Multi-Device Pairing**
One Brick can pair with multiple phones, and one phone can pair with multiple Bricks, useful for families or anyone who wants a spare.

## Brick App Review: Does Strict Mode Actually Hold Up?

Here's where things play out in practice. The tap ritual works exactly as advertised for starting a block, and ending one on purpose, when you legitimately need an app, is just as easy. The problem reviewers flag is the in between case: someone trying to defeat Strict Mode without the Brick in hand. At least one App Store reviewer, C Parlee, says that's possible in a single step through iOS's own Screen Time settings, which undercuts the "you literally can't get back in" pitch for anyone determined enough to dig into their phone's settings menu.

## Who Is Brick Best For?

**People who respond to physical, not digital, friction.** If software toggles have never worked because you just tap through them, moving the off switch to an object you have to walk to is a real behavior change.

**Anyone tired of subscriptions.** A one time hardware purchase with a free companion app is unusual in this category, where most competitors bill monthly or yearly.

**Families or households sharing one Brick.** Since one device pairs with multiple phones, a household can share a single physical Brick for shared phones down time.

## Who Should Look Elsewhere?

**Anyone who travels light or loses small objects easily.** The whole system depends on having the Brick with you, or intentionally leaving it somewhere, at the right moment.

**Android users who need website blocking.** That feature isn't supported on Android yet, per review coverage.

**People who want the block to be truly unbreakable.** Strict Mode is a real deterrent, but it's been reported bypassable through iOS's own settings by at least one reviewer, so treat it as a strong nudge, not an unbeatable lock.

**Anyone who wants detailed screen time analytics.** Brick's reporting is limited to daily usage duration.

## Final Verdict

Brick earns its reputation as one of the more physically honest tools in this category. The tap ritual is real friction, not a suggestion, and for a lot of people that's exactly the push they need. If what you're really after is a reason to put the phone down that's rooted in something beyond willpower, this Brick app review's honest conclusion is simple: try the physical ritual if you think it'll stick, and consider FaithLock if you'd rather that moment of friction be a piece of Scripture instead of an object on your desk.
