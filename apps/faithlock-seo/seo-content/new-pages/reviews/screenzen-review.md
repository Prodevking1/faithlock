## What Is ScreenZen?

ScreenZen, from ScreenZen LLC, takes a friction-first approach to screen time. Instead of an outright block, it inserts a customizable delay before a chosen app or website opens, with the wait time increasing the more you try to open it. It also interrupts endless scrolling by limiting sessions to short bursts, and shows motivational or reflective prompts before granting access.

On iOS, ScreenZen requires Screen Time and Shortcuts permissions, consistent with Apple's underlying Family Controls framework. On Android, it uses Accessibility Service permissions instead.

This ScreenZen review looks at whether that delay mechanic, and the app's free pricing, holds up once you factor in the sourced weaknesses below.

## Is ScreenZen Free?

**Yes. ScreenZen is free with optional donations, and no mandatory subscription was found.** The one financial catch: if you enable the app's uninstall-prevention or lock feature and then forget your passcode, at least one Trustpilot reviewer reports that unlocking your own settings can require a paid unlock request.

For most users who don't lock themselves out, ScreenZen genuinely costs nothing to use.

## How It Works

1. **Download** ScreenZen on iOS, iPadOS, macOS, or Android
2. **Grant Screen Time and Shortcuts permissions** on iOS, or Accessibility Service permissions on Android
3. **Choose apps or sites** to add a delay to
4. When you try to open one, ScreenZen shows a **customizable delay**, with wait times that increase the more often you try
5. During the wait, you may see a **motivational or reflective prompt**
6. Set **per-app daily usage limits**, which block access entirely once you hit your goal for the day
7. Optionally enable a **passcode and lock mode** to prevent yourself from disabling the settings mid session

The delay itself starts short and grows with repeated attempts, a design meant to make repeated app-checking progressively more annoying.
