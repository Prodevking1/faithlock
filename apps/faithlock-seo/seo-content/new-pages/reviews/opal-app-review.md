## What Is Opal?

Opal, from Opal OS Corporation, is a screen time and focus app built on top of Apple's Screen Time API. It blocks distracting apps during Focus Sessions, tracks a Focus Score, and layers on rewards, milestones, and a community leaderboard for people who like a bit of competition with their self control.

The headline feature is Deep Focus: once you start a Deep Focus session, you can't cancel it early. That non-negotiable structure is Opal's answer to the "just turn it off for a second" problem that undermines most blockers.

This Opal app review breaks down what's actually included at each pricing tier, since that turns out to matter a lot here.

---

## Is Opal Free?

**Opal has a free tier, but it's limited to one recurring session and excludes Deep Focus mode entirely.** An independent review published August 15, 2026 calls the free plan almost useless for that reason. Getting the full feature set, including Deep Focus, requires the $99.99 per year Pro subscription or a one time lifetime purchase.

If you just want to try Opal's basic scheduling, the free tier works. If Deep Focus is the reason you're considering Opal (and for most people, it is), plan on paying.

## How It Works

1. **Download** Opal on iOS, Mac, or as a Chrome extension
2. **Grant Screen Time permissions** through Apple's Family Controls framework
3. **Set up a Focus Session**, scheduled or recurring, choosing which apps and sites to block
4. **Start Deep Focus** if you want a session you can't cancel early
5. Optionally enable a **Screen Time Passcode and App Uninstall Protection** to stop yourself from tampering with settings mid session
6. Review your **Focus Score and Focus Report** afterward
7. Earn **rewards and milestones**, and optionally compete on the community leaderboard

Recurring sessions can sync to your calendar or iOS Focus Mode, so Opal can trigger automatically without you remembering to start it.

## Features Breakdown

**Focus Sessions**
Timed blocking of apps and websites, with scheduled breaks built in. You can set these to recur automatically.

**Deep Focus**
A non-cancellable blocking mode. Once the timer starts, there's no early exit button. This is Opal's strictest and most distinctive feature.

**Focus Score and Reports**
Analytics on how consistently you're using the app and staying off blocked apps, presented as a score you can track over time.

**Rewards, Milestones, and Leaderboard**
Gamified elements including a community leaderboard where you can invite friends, useful if social accountability motivates you more than solo willpower.

**Anti-Bypass Options**
A Screen Time Passcode and App Uninstall Protection setting exist specifically to make it harder to disable Opal mid session. Opal's own help center is upfront that without these enabled, the app can be turned off in one step via iOS Settings.

## Opal App Review: Is the Anti-Bypass Protection Reliable?

The honest answer, per Opal's own documentation, is: only if you turn it on. Screen Time Passcode and App Uninstall Protection are opt in settings, not defaults. Without them, Opal's help center itself states the app can be disabled in one step through iOS Settings. With them enabled, an independent review still found that determined users could delete the app, change phone settings, or otherwise work around Deep Focus. Opal is a strong nudge with real optional friction, but it isn't presented as an unbeatable lock, and the reviews back that up.
