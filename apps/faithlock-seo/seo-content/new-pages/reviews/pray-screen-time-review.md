## What Is Pray Screen Time?

Pray Screen Time is a faith-based app blocker that doesn't try to do too much. The concept: you select apps you want to block, and when you try to open them, you pray first. That's it.

No scanning, no quizzes, no mood journals, no AI Bible chat. Just a prayer prompt standing between you and your distractions. Every morning, you get a reminder to start your day with prayer, plus a daily Bible verse to set the tone.

What makes Pray Screen Time notable isn't its features — it's its availability. In a category dominated by iOS-only apps, Pray Screen Time works on both iOS and Android. For the millions of Christian Android users who've been locked out of faith-based blocking tools, this is a big deal.

---

## How It Works

1. **Download** from the App Store or Google Play
2. **Select the apps** you want to block (social media, games, news, whatever consumes you)
3. **Grant the necessary permissions** — Screen Time on iOS, Accessibility/Usage Access on Android
4. When you open a blocked app, a **prayer prompt** appears
5. **Spend a moment in prayer** — the app gives you a brief prompt or passage to reflect on
6. After engaging with the prayer, your app **unlocks**
7. Each morning, receive a **prayer reminder** and **daily verse**

The prayer prompts are short and accessible — not lengthy devotionals. Think 15-30 seconds of intentional pause, not a full quiet time. The idea is friction, not a Bible study session.

## Features Breakdown

**Prayer-Based Unlocking**
The core feature. When you try to open Instagram or YouTube, a prayer prompt appears first. The prompts rotate and cover themes like gratitude, patience, focus, and surrender. They're short enough that you won't resent them, but long enough to interrupt the autopilot of mindless scrolling.

**Morning Prayer Reminders**
Each morning, you get a notification encouraging you to start your day with prayer. It's gentle — not the aggressive "YOU HAVEN'T PRAYED TODAY" guilt trip some apps deliver. Just a nudge.

**Daily Bible Verse**
A fresh verse each day, displayed in the app and sometimes integrated into the unlock experience. The verse selection leans toward encouragement and wisdom — Psalms, Proverbs, and Paul's letters feature heavily.

**App Selection**
You choose exactly which apps to block. The interface is straightforward: see your installed apps, tap the ones you want to block, done. You can adjust anytime.

**Android Support**
This is the feature that matters most for a huge chunk of users. On Android, the blocking uses Accessibility Services, which is a different technical approach than Apple's Screen Time API. It works, but with some quirks (more on that in the cons).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Pray Screen Time really free?**
The core blocking feature is free and fully functional. Premium exists for extra prayer content and customization, but you don't need it to block apps and receive prayer prompts. This is one of the most generous free tiers in the faith-based blocker category.

**How does it work on Android vs iOS?**
On iOS, it uses Apple's Screen Time API (Family Controls), which is system-level and very hard to bypass. On Android, it uses Accessibility Services, which works but is technically easier to circumvent. Some Android users report bugs including getting stuck in demo loops and needing to restart in safe mode.

**Can I block specific apps or is it all-or-nothing?**
You choose exactly which apps to block. Most people block social media (Instagram, TikTok, X/Twitter, YouTube) and games, but you can select any app on your phone.

**Will it conflict with my existing Screen Time settings?**
On iOS, Pray Screen Time works alongside Apple's built-in Screen Time — they're complementary. Your existing Screen Time limits stay in place. On Android, it works alongside Digital Wellbeing without conflict.

**How long are the prayer prompts?**
Short. Plan for 15-30 seconds. They're not full devotionals — they're brief pauses designed to interrupt your autopilot and redirect your attention to God. If you want longer, more immersive content, look at Holy Focus or Sanctum.

**Is it safe to give Accessibility permissions on Android?**
Accessibility Services is a broad Android permission that allows apps to interact with other apps. This is how all third-party app blockers work on Android. Pray Screen Time uses it solely for blocking — but it's worth knowing the permission exists. Only grant it to apps you trust.

**My app is stuck in a demo loop — what do I do?**
This is a known Android issue some users report. Try force-stopping the app, clearing its cache, and restarting. In some cases, users have needed to restart their phone in safe mode to resolve it. The developers have addressed this in updates, but it still surfaces occasionally.

**Does it work with WhatsApp and messaging apps?**
Yes, you can block any app on your phone, including messaging apps. Be aware that blocking communication apps means you'll need to pray before responding to messages, which could be inconvenient in time-sensitive situations.
