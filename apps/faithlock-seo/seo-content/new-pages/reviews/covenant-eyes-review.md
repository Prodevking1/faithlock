## What Is Covenant Eyes?

Covenant Eyes, now marketed under the name Victory by Covenant Eyes, is a screen accountability service built around a specific idea: instead of trying to block explicit content outright, it shows your activity to someone you trust. You pick an accountability partner, your activity gets reported to them on a schedule or in real time, and the theory is that knowing someone is watching changes behavior more reliably than a filter that can be turned off in private.

This review covers what Covenant Eyes actually does on each platform, where its iOS coverage falls short compared to Android, Windows, and macOS, what it costs, and who it genuinely fits.

---

## How It Works

1. **Choose an accountability partner**, a spouse, friend, mentor, or someone from a recovery group
2. **Install Covenant Eyes** on your devices, iOS, Android, Windows, or macOS
3. On **Android, Windows, and macOS**, the app takes regular screenshots, analyzed by AI to flag explicit content
4. Those flagged results are compiled into a **report sent to your accountability partner**, either as alerts or a scheduled summary
5. On **iOS**, the mechanism is different: a **filtering VPN** blocks adult content at the network level, and a **Safari extension** captures screenshots inside Safari specifically
6. Your partner can follow up directly with you based on what the report shows

Covenant Eyes doesn't use Apple's Screen Time API at all. It relies on its own VPN filter and screenshot capture on iOS, which is a meaningfully different mechanism than a Screen Time based app blocker.

## Features Breakdown

**AI Screenshot Analysis**
On Android, Windows, and macOS, Covenant Eyes periodically screenshots the device and runs the images through AI trained to flag explicit content. This is the core of the service on those platforms and the basis for the reports sent to your accountability partner.

**iOS Content-Filtering VPN**
On iPhone, Covenant Eyes takes a different approach: a VPN profile filters adult content at the network level. This runs independently of the screenshot mechanism used elsewhere, since Apple restricts what third-party apps can capture on iOS.

**Safari-Only Screenshot Extension**
Alongside the VPN, an iOS Safari extension captures screenshots, but only within Safari. Other browsers and apps on the same iPhone aren't covered by this screenshot mechanism, which is the most-cited gap in independent coverage of the iOS version.

**Accountability Partner Reports**
This is the actual product, more than the technology underneath it. Reports go to a person you've chosen, not to a company dashboard you check yourself. The theory, and the reason Covenant Eyes has stayed relevant for years, is that knowing a specific person will see your activity changes behavior more than a private filter does.

**Strive and Arise Recovery Programs**
Covenant Eyes offers Strive for men and Arise for women, structured recovery programs that go beyond the monitoring product itself. This is a genuine differentiator from apps that are pure filters or pure monitors with no program attached.

**Household Coverage**
One subscription covers up to 10 users and unlimited devices in a household, which changes the value calculation for families or larger accountability groups compared to per-device pricing elsewhere.

## Who Should Look Elsewhere?

**Anyone assuming Covenant Eyes fully monitors an iPhone.** It doesn't. The Safari-only screenshot coverage and lack of Screen Time integration mean other browsers and apps on iOS aren't covered the way they are on Android, Windows, or macOS.

**People without anyone willing to be their accountability partner.** The reporting model depends on an engaged person on the other end. Without one, you're paying for a screenshot service nobody is actually reviewing.

**Budget-conscious users who want a free tier to start with.** Covenant Eyes offers a 30-day trial, not an ongoing free plan. If you want to test a concept before paying anything long-term, that trial window is your only free option.

**People who want the content actually blocked, not just reported.** Outside its narrower iOS VPN filter, Covenant Eyes' Android, Windows, and macOS approach documents activity after the fact rather than preventing access to it in the moment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does Covenant Eyes cost?**
There's no permanent free tier. After a 30-day trial with a money-back guarantee, pricing runs monthly, annually, or as a one-time lifetime purchase, with one plan covering up to 10 users and unlimited devices per household. Check covenanteyes.com/pricing for current figures, since subscription pricing changes.

**Does Covenant Eyes have a free trial?**
Yes, 30 days, backed by a money-back guarantee if you cancel within that window.

**Is Covenant Eyes worth it for iPhone users specifically?**
It depends on what you need. The iOS version's screenshot coverage is limited to Safari, and there's no Screen Time integration, which is a real gap compared to Android, Windows, or macOS. If your main concern is content encountered outside Safari, on other browsers or in other apps, Covenant Eyes' iOS coverage won't catch it the way its desktop and Android versions do.

**Can Covenant Eyes see everything I do on my phone?**
Not on iPhone. Outside its VPN-based content filter, screenshot capture on iOS only works inside Safari. On Android, Windows, and macOS, screenshot coverage is broader across the device.

**Does Covenant Eyes work without an accountability partner actually reviewing the reports?**
Technically the software keeps running, but most of its actual value depends on a partner engaging with what it reports. Without that, this Covenant Eyes review would come down to: you're paying for screenshots nobody is looking at.

**Is Covenant Eyes better than a straightforward app or content blocker?**
It depends on what you're trying to solve. Covenant Eyes' accountability-partner reporting model addresses a different problem than a technical block does. If you want content actually intercepted rather than reported afterward, a filtering tool like Canopy is closer to that. If you want your own apps blocked outright with a faith-based unlock step, that's closer to what FaithLock does, though it's a different category entirely from accountability reporting.

## Final Verdict

Covenant Eyes isn't trying to be a hard technical block, and judged against that expectation it will disappoint you, especially on iPhone, where its coverage is genuinely thinner than on other platforms. Judged as an accountability-partner service with structured recovery programs attached, it's a more complete offering than most competitors in that specific lane, and the Strive and Arise programs are a real differentiator.

If what you actually need is a person who sees your activity and holds you to it, and you're prepared to keep that partner engaged, Covenant Eyes does that job. If you were hoping for a filter that quietly blocks content without involving anyone else, or full coverage across every app and browser on an iPhone, look elsewhere first.
