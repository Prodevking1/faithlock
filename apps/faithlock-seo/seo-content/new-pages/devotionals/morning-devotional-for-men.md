## What You Do First Decides a Lot

There is a reason so much of Scripture happens at dawn. The first hour of a man's day is the only hour he tends to have any real control over. By 9 AM your time belongs to other people. Before that, it is yours, and what you put into it tends to set the shape of everything after.

For most men the honest problem is not that mornings are unavailable. It is that they are already spent. The alarm goes off, the phone is in your hand within thirty seconds, and by the time you are upright you have already absorbed a dozen inputs you did not choose. The day starts reactive and never recovers.

These six readings are about that first hour specifically. Each takes a few minutes and ends with something to do before work starts.

## Reading 1: Say Something Before You Read Anything

> "In the morning, LORD, you hear my voice; in the morning I lay my requests before you and wait expectantly." (Psalm 5:3, NIV)

David says "in the morning" twice in one sentence. The repetition is the point. This is not a man who prays when he gets around to it. It is a fixed position in the day.

Look at the order: he speaks, then he waits expectantly. Most men do the opposite. They consume first and speak later, if at all. Reversing that order changes who is setting the agenda for the next sixteen hours.

**Before work today:** Speak one sentence to God out loud before you look at a single screen. Out loud matters. It is harder to do absent-mindedly.

## Reading 2: Jesus Got Up Early to Be Alone

> "Very early in the morning, while it was still dark, Jesus got up, left the house and went off to a solitary place, where he prayed." (Mark 1:35, NIV)

This comes right after a day where Jesus healed a town's worth of sick people until late at night. He had every reason to sleep in. He got up in the dark instead.

Three deliberate actions: got up, left the house, went off to a solitary place. He created physical distance from the people who needed him. If the man with the shortest ministry window in history budgeted time to be alone with the Father, the argument that you are too busy does not hold up well.

**Before work today:** Find your solitary place. A parked car counts. A garage counts. Ten minutes where nobody can reach you.

## Reading 3: Ask Before Anything Has Gone Wrong

> "I rise before dawn and cry for help; I have put my hope in your word." (Psalm 119:147, NIV)

"Cry for help" is not a phrase most men use about themselves. It sounds like weakness. But the psalmist writes it without apology, and he writes it before dawn, which means it is the first thing on his mind rather than a last resort after everything else failed.

Most men wait until a situation is unmanageable before asking for help, from God or anyone. The psalmist has inverted this. He asks first, while the day is still theoretical, before anything has gone wrong.

**Before work today:** Ask for help with something that has not gone wrong yet. Name the meeting, the conversation, the temptation you can see coming.

## Reading 4: Guard the Inputs

> "Above all else, guard your heart, for everything you do flows from it." (Proverbs 4:23, NIV)

"Above all else" is a ranking. Of everything a man could prioritize, Solomon puts this first, ahead of work, money, and reputation.

"Guard" is a military word. It assumes something is trying to get in. And the reason given is causal: everything you do flows from it. Your heart is upstream of your behavior, which means the fight over your character happens long before the moment you fail. It happens at the inputs. What you let into your mind in the first hour of the day is not a neutral choice.

**Question for today:** What gets into you between waking and leaving the house? List it honestly. That list is your actual morning devotional, whether you chose it or not.

## Reading 5: Ask for Satisfaction Early

> "Satisfy us in the morning with your unfailing love, that we may sing for joy and be glad all our days." (Psalm 90:14, NIV)

This is a request for satisfaction at the start rather than the end. Most men chase satisfaction through the day and hope to reach it by evening: close the deal, finish the list, get the recognition. Moses asks to be satisfied in the morning, before any of it.

That order matters, because a man who starts the day satisfied works differently from one who is working to become satisfied. The first does his job. The second needs his job to tell him who he is.

**Before work today:** Ask to be satisfied before you have accomplished anything. Notice how uncomfortable that is, and do it anyway.

## Reading 6: Do Not Just Read It

> "Do not merely listen to the word, and so deceive yourselves. Do what it says. Anyone who listens to the word but does not do what it says is like someone who looks at his face in a mirror and, after looking at himself, goes away and immediately forgets what he looks like." (James 1:22-24, NIV)

The mirror image is exact and slightly humiliating. A man looks, sees something true about himself, walks away, and forgets within seconds. That is what a devotional becomes when there is no action attached.

"So deceive yourselves" is the sharp part. James says reading without doing does not leave you neutral. It leaves you deceived, because you now feel like a man who takes Scripture seriously while behaving like one who does not.

**Before work today:** Take the one action from whichever reading hit hardest this week. Not the easiest one. The one you have been mentally filing away.

## How to Actually Get Up and Do This

**Move the phone out of the bedroom.** This is the single highest-leverage change most men can make, and it is the one they resist hardest. If the phone charges in the kitchen, you have to be standing before you can scroll. That one physical fact wins most of the fight.

**Decide the night before.** Set the Bible out. Open it to the passage. Put the coffee ready. Morning-you is not making good decisions at 5:40 AM, so make them at 10 PM instead.

**Use the same trigger every day.** Coffee brewing. The dog going out. The truck warming up. Attach the reading to something that already happens without willpower.

**Start with ten minutes, not an hour.** Men routinely announce a 5 AM two-hour regimen on Monday and are asleep through the alarm by Thursday. Ten minutes you keep beats ninety you abandon.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What time should a man do his morning devotional?**
Whatever time is genuinely quiet and repeatable for you. The specific hour matters far less than doing it before the day's demands start arriving. For most men that is before the household wakes or before leaving for work.

**How long should a morning devotional be?**
Ten to fifteen minutes is a sustainable target. Long enough to actually think about a passage, short enough that you will still do it on a bad week.

**What if I am not a morning person?**
Then do it later and stop feeling guilty about it. Morning devotions are a strong default, not a rule. Our [daily devotional for men](/resources/daily-devotional-for-men) works at any hour, and plenty of men do better with an [evening devotional](/resources/evening-devotional) after the house is quiet.

**Should I pray or read Scripture first?**
Psalm 5:3 suggests speaking first, then waiting. In practice most men find a short prayer before reading clears the mental clutter enough to actually absorb the text. Try it in that order for a week and see.

**How do I stop checking my phone before my devotional?**
Change the environment rather than trying harder. Charge the phone in another room, or put an app blocker on your feeds until a set hour. Both approaches beat resolving to resist, because they remove the decision instead of testing it.
