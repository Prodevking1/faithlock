## Quick Answer

The Opal screen time app blocks distracting apps and sites through Apple's Screen Time API on iPhone, iPad, and Mac. Setup runs about five minutes: install it, grant Screen Time permission, build a Focus Session with the apps you want gone, then turn on Deep Focus if you want a session you cannot cancel. The step almost everyone skips is enabling the Screen Time Passcode and App Uninstall Protection, and without those two settings Opal's own help center says the app can be switched off in one step.

*This page is a configuration guide for people already using Opal or about to install it. For whether Opal is worth its price against the alternatives, see the [Opal app review](/resources/opal-app-review), which carries the verdict.*

## What Opal Actually Is

Opal, from Opal OS Corporation, runs on Apple's Screen Time API, the same Family Controls framework most serious iPhone blockers are built on. It covers iPhone, iPad, and Mac natively, adds a Chrome extension for browser blocking, and has an Android version that carries ads on the free tier and weaker blocking due to platform limitations.

What it adds on top of the blocking is measurement and social pressure: a Focus Score, focus reports, rewards, milestones, and a community leaderboard you can invite friends to. If you like seeing progress as a number, this is the deepest reporting in the category.

## Setting Up the Opal Screen Time App

### 1. Install and grant Screen Time access

Download Opal on iPhone, iPad, or Mac, and approve the Screen Time permission when prompted. This is what lets Opal enforce anything. If you decline it, the app becomes a dashboard.

### 2. Build your first Focus Session

A Focus Session is a timed block of chosen apps and websites, with scheduled breaks built in. Start narrow. Pick the two or three apps that actually cost you hours, not every app you feel vaguely guilty about. Broad first sessions are the most common reason people abandon a blocker in week one.

### 3. Make it recurring

Recurring sessions are where Opal separates from simpler tools. They can sync to your calendar and to iOS Focus Mode, so blocking triggers when a meeting begins or when you switch your phone into Work Focus, rather than at a hardcoded hour you will eventually resent. Set these up once and stop making the decision daily.

### 4. Turn on Deep Focus when you mean it

Deep Focus is Opal's signature feature: once the timer starts, the session cannot be cancelled early. There is no button. Use it for the blocks that matter and leave ordinary sessions cancellable, otherwise you will lock yourself out of something you genuinely need and lose trust in the whole setup.

Note that Deep Focus is a paid feature. An independent review published March 2026, checked 2026-08-15 states the free plan excludes it almost entirely, which makes the free tier close to a demo rather than a trial.

### 5. Enable both anti-bypass settings

This is the step that changes whether Opal works on you.

- **Screen Time Passcode**: locks Opal's Screen Time access behind a passcode
- **App Uninstall Protection**: prevents deleting the app during a session

Opal's own help center is upfront that without these enabled, the app can be turned off in one step via iOS Settings, Screen Time, by toggling off "Apps with Screen Time Access." Turning both on is the single highest-value five minutes you will spend in this app.

The stronger version of this: have someone else enter the Screen Time passcode. A passcode you chose is a passcode you remember at 11:40pm.

## Reading Your Focus Score Without Lying to Yourself

The Focus Score and focus report are Opal's best non-blocking feature, and the most common way people misuse them is treating the number as the goal. A rising score with no change in how the evenings feel usually means the sessions are scheduled around hours you were never going to scroll anyway.

Two habits make the data useful:

**Compare weekday evenings to weekend evenings.** Most people's real problem lives in one of those two buckets, and an average across the week hides it.

**Watch the sessions you cancel, not the ones you complete.** A completed session tells you the schedule was easy. A cancelled one tells you where the actual pressure is, which is where the next Deep Focus block should go.

## What the Opal Screen Time App Will Not Do, However You Configure It

Everything above is a setting you can change. These are not, so plan around them rather than hunting for a toggle:

- **Deep Focus is not unbeatable.** The same independent review that priced Pro found users can still delete the app, change phone settings, or find workarounds even with Deep Focus enabled, concluding the software can be bypassed by the person it is trying to help.
- **The free tier is thin.** One recurring session and no Deep Focus, per that review, which calls the free plan almost useless for that reason.
- **The price is high for the category.** The same review calls $99.99 per year steep for a screen time blocker. A lifetime purchase option exists, though the amount is not independently verified with a dated source here.
- **Android is the weaker build.** Ads on the free tier and blocking that is not as robust as iOS, due to platform limitations.
- **Billing complaints exist.** A user on Opal's own community forum reported the app blocking apps they had not selected, an easy bypass through Screen Time settings, and three days of effort to get a refund after an unwanted renewal.

## Getting More Out of It

**Do not run a second Screen Time blocker alongside it.** Jomo's public troubleshooting documentation names conflicts with other Screen Time apps as a leading cause of blocks failing to engage or failing to release. Two blockers is worse than one.

**Use the leaderboard only if competition actually motivates you.** For some people the friend leaderboard is the reason the habit sticks. For others it becomes another feed to check, which is a strange outcome for a screen time tool.

**Schedule around your calendar, not your intentions.** The calendar integration is the most underused feature here and the one least dependent on your willpower.

**Decide what the blocked time is for.** Opal is very good at removing something. It is not designed to put anything in its place, which is fine if you already know what you are doing with the hour.

## Alternatives Worth Considering

**Jomo**, if Opal's price is the sticking point. It runs on the same Screen Time API with a Strict Mode that adds a multi-day lockout and anti-uninstall protection, priced at $5.99 per month, $29.99 per year, or $99.99 lifetime per its App Store listing checked 2026-08-15. **Freedom**, if the laptop is as much of a problem as the phone. One synced block list covers iOS, Android, macOS, Windows, and the browser. Freedom's own documentation notes that on iOS its Locked Mode does not stop you disabling the Screen Time permission unless a separate passcode is set. It uses the same Family Controls (Screen Time) API Opal uses, and instead of a paid strict mode standing between you and a blocked app, there is a Bible verse from the complete BSB library of 31,000+ verses and a question about what you just read. Scheduled lock times cover morning devotion, work hours, and bedtime. It is iOS only, it is a self-accountability tool rather than a parental control, and the mechanism only makes sense if Scripture is something you want in that moment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the Opal screen time app free?**
There is a free tier, but an independent review published March 2026, checked 2026-08-15 describes it as limited to one recurring session with no Deep Focus, and calls it almost useless for that reason. Deep Focus, which is why most people install Opal, requires the Pro subscription, priced at $99.99 per year per that review, with a lifetime option also available.

**How do I stop myself from turning Opal off?**
Enable both Screen Time Passcode and App Uninstall Protection in Opal's settings. Opal's own help center states that without them the app can be disabled in one step via iOS Settings, Screen Time. For this to actually hold, have someone else enter the passcode.

**Can Deep Focus be cancelled early?**
Not through Opal itself; that is the defining feature. An independent review did find that users could still delete the app, change phone settings, or find other workarounds even with Deep Focus running, so treat it as very strong friction rather than a guarantee.

**Does the Opal screen time app work on Android?**
There is an Android version, described as having ads on the free tier and blocking that is not as robust as on iOS, due to platform limitations. If Android is your main device, Opal is not the strongest choice available.

**What is a cheaper alternative to the Opal screen time app?**
Jomo offers a comparable Screen Time API foundation with Strict Mode at a substantially lower published price, and ScreenZen is free with optional donations. Both are covered in our roundup of the [best app blockers for iPhone](/resources/best-app-blockers-iphone) and the [best screen time management apps](/resources/screen-time-management-app).
