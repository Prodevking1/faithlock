## Key Takeaways

- **Deepest scheduling on iPhone**: Jomo, which blocks by schedule, by usage limit, or until you complete an action
- **Best triggers beyond the clock**: AppBlock, which can start a block by time, day, location, or Wi-Fi network
- **Best synced schedules**: Freedom, where one recurring session applies to phone, laptop, and browser at once
- **Best on a computer**: Cold Turkey Blocker, whose Frozen Turkey lock cannot be lifted before its deadline
- **Best calendar integration**: Opal, whose recurring sessions hook into your calendar and iOS Focus Mode
- **Best for devotion and bedtime windows**: FaithLock, with scheduled lock times and a Bible quiz to reopen anything early
- **Skip it if scheduling is your priority**: Brick, whose scheduler cannot auto-end a session after a fixed duration, per an independent review

## Why a Time Blocker App Beats a Willpower Blocker

An on-demand blocker asks you to make the right decision at the exact moment you are least equipped to make it. You have to notice the urge, name it, open a second app, and start a session, all while the first app is one tap away.

A time blocker app removes that step. The rule fires at 9pm because it is 9pm. You made the decision once, calmly, days ago, and the version of you at 11:40pm does not get a vote.

That is the whole argument for scheduling, and it is why the apps below are ranked by how much control they give you over *when*, rather than how hard they hit. A blocker with a great lock and a weak scheduler will still lose to the version of you that never turns it on.

## The 7 Best Time Blocker Apps

### 1. Jomo

**Best for**: The most precise schedules you can build on an iPhone

Jomo, from Jomo SAS, is built on Apple's Screen Time API for iPhone, iPad, and Mac. Blocks can run on a schedule, on a usage limit, or "until you take an action," and you can block whole categories (social media, games, shopping) rather than adding apps one at a time. Strict Mode extends a lockout across multiple days with no early opt-out, and turns on anti-uninstall protection while it runs.

**Scheduling depth**: The strongest on iPhone. Three separate trigger types, category groups, and a multi-day lock that survives your second thoughts.

**Where it falls short**: Jomo publishes a troubleshooting article for blocks that fail to engage or fail to release on schedule, which opens with "we understand your frustration and share it." It names conflicts with other Screen Time apps, Low Power Mode, and outdated software as causes. If two blockers are installed at once, expect friction. A published review also found the free version too limited, with scheduled sessions and usage insights paywalled, and noted Jomo cannot block Safari at the granular level Apple's own tools allow.

### 2. AppBlock

**Best for**: Blocks that fire on where you are, not only when it is

AppBlock, from MobileSoft s.r.o., is the only app here whose schedules can key off location and Wi-Fi network alongside time and day. Arrive at the office, social media locks. Connect to the home network after 10pm, the feed apps go away. It also offers Quick Block for one-off sessions, an allowlist mode that blocks everything except your chosen apps, and browser extensions for Chrome, Edge, and Brave.

**Scheduling depth**: The most trigger types of anything on this list. Time, day, location, and network.

**Where it falls short**: On iPhone specifically, a user reported that revoking AppBlock's access under Settings, Screen Time disables Strict Mode entirely, and the developer confirmed in a reply that this is an iOS restriction they cannot fix. The iOS free tier is tight (a 4-hour Quick Block plus two schedules). Reviewers report notifications reappearing several times an hour after being disabled, pauses taking up to 30 seconds to apply, and the app forgetting a paid subscription until restarted. AppBlock is primarily an Android product, and the iOS version has real feature gaps.

### 3. Freedom

**Best for**: One schedule that covers the phone and the laptop simultaneously

Freedom is the only tool here where a recurring session applies everywhere at once. On iOS it combines the Screen Time API for app blocking with a local VPN profile for cross-browser website blocking, and the same block list runs on Android, macOS, Windows, and in Chrome, Safari, and Firefox. Locked Mode prevents editing or stopping an active session, with one early exit permitted every seven days.

**Scheduling depth**: Strong, and uniquely cross-device. If your 9-to-5 block only covers your phone, you will simply use the laptop, which is exactly the hole Freedom closes.

**Where it falls short**: Freedom's own support documentation states that on iOS, Locked Mode does not by itself stop you from disabling Freedom's Screen Time permission in Settings, unless you also set a separate Screen Time passcode. App Store reviews report a limited block accidentally blocking "the entirety of the internet," and one iPhone left unusable until reinstalled. Trustpilot reviews report refund difficulties and billing described as not transparent.

### 4. Cold Turkey Blocker

**Best for**: Scheduled blocks on a Windows machine, with no way out

Cold Turkey Blocker, from Cold Turkey Software, is a desktop tool with recurring weekly scheduling and the strictest lock in this article. Frozen Turkey mode means that once a block starts, it cannot be stopped before the timer expires, the app cannot be uninstalled while locked, and Block Strictness settings prevent switching to an unsupported browser or changing the system clock or region to cheat the schedule. It blocks websites and desktop applications, and Pro is a one-time purchase with no subscription.

**Scheduling depth**: Excellent on Windows, and the anti-cheat measures around the schedule (clock changes, browser switching) are unmatched here.

**Where it falls short**: There is no mobile version at all, so it does nothing about your phone and you will need a second tool. On Mac, reviews report blocks being "often delayed and sometimes completely non-functional" because the architecture runs into macOS sandboxing, with one user reporting a slowdown severe enough that simple programs took up to 10 minutes to open after installation. The interface is described as dated, and the free version as very limited next to Pro.

### 5. Opal

**Best for**: Schedules that follow your calendar instead of a fixed clock

Opal's recurring sessions can sync to your calendar and to iOS Focus Mode, so blocking triggers when a meeting or a Focus starts rather than at a hardcoded hour. Deep Focus makes a session non-cancellable once the timer runs, and the Focus Score and focus reports show whether your schedule is actually holding.

**Scheduling depth**: Strong, with the best contextual integration here. Calendar-driven blocking is genuinely different from time-driven blocking.

**Where it falls short**: An independent review published March 2026, checked 2026-08-15, calls the $99.99 per year Pro price steep for a screen time blocker and says the free plan is almost useless because it excludes Deep Focus. The same review found workarounds available even with Deep Focus enabled. Opal's own help center states that without a Screen Time Passcode and App Uninstall Protection enabled, the app can be turned off in one step through iOS Settings.

### 6. FaithLock

**Best for**: Protecting a morning devotion, a work block, and a bedtime window

FaithLock schedules lock times around three windows most people already care about: morning devotion, work hours, and bedtime. Blocking runs through Apple's Family Controls (Screen Time) API, the same layer Jomo and Opal use. What happens if you need something inside a locked window is the part that differs. You get a Bible verse and a fill-in-the-blank quiz question about it, pulled from the complete BSB (Berean Standard Bible) library of 31,000+ verses, and answering correctly is how you get in.

Prayer reminders run through the day, and streak tracking with badges and a streak-freeze system records whether the schedule is holding.

**Scheduling depth**: Focused rather than exhaustive. Three named windows aimed at a specific rhythm, not a general-purpose rule builder like Jomo's or AppBlock's.

**Where it falls short**: iOS only, with no Android version. No location or Wi-Fi triggers, and no calendar integration. It is a self-accountability tool with no parental control or family account features. And the quiz only functions as friction if Scripture is something you want to spend thirty seconds on, which is a genuine filter on who this suits.

### 7. ScreenZen

**Best for**: Scheduled daily limits without paying anything

ScreenZen is free with optional donations. It sets per-app daily usage limits and blocks once a goal is reached, and those limits are schedulable by day and by time. On top of that sit escalating delays before an app opens, session-length caps to break long scrolls, and reflective prompts.

**Scheduling depth**: Solid for a free tool, though it is built around daily budgets more than around hard time windows.

**Where it falls short**: A Trustpilot reviewer states ScreenZen sends screen recordings and third-party app activity to its servers without this being mentioned in the privacy policy, and reports receiving no response from support after asking. Another reports that forgetting the app passcode after enabling uninstall prevention locks you out of your settings until a paid unlock request is processed. An independent review site lists "can be bypassed" as a known weak point.

## A Note on Brick, and Why It Is Not on This List

Brick is a good product and a poor time blocker. Its NFC hardware means a session starts and ends with a physical tap, and an independent review notes the scheduler cannot auto-end a session after a fixed duration. App Store reviewers also report scheduled unblocking sometimes failing to trigger, requiring a phone restart as a workaround.

If you want a physical ritual, Brick is worth a look, and we cover it properly in the [Brick app blocker guide](/resources/brick-app-blocker-guide). If you specifically want blocks that run on a clock without you touching anything, pick from the seven above instead.

## How to Choose a Time Blocker App

**You want maximum scheduling control on iPhone**: Jomo.

**Your problem is contextual, not chronological (the office, the couch, the bedroom)**: AppBlock, for its location and Wi-Fi triggers.

**Blocking your phone just pushes you to the laptop**: Freedom.

**The laptop is the whole problem and you are on Windows**: Cold Turkey Blocker.

**Your day is already run by your calendar**: Opal.

**You want the locked window to be devotional rather than merely empty**: FaithLock.

**You will not pay for this**: ScreenZen.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a time blocker app?**
A time blocker app enforces app or website restrictions on a schedule you set in advance, rather than requiring you to start a session manually. The rule fires at the hour you chose, whether or not you feel like honoring it that day, which is the entire point.

**What is the best free time blocker app?**
ScreenZen, which is free with optional donations and lets you schedule per-app daily limits by day and time. Apple's built-in Screen Time also does scheduled Downtime for free, though its Ignore Limit button undercuts it for most people.

**Can I schedule blocks by location instead of time?**
AppBlock is the one app here that supports it, with triggers based on location and Wi-Fi network alongside time and day. Opal takes a related approach through calendar and iOS Focus Mode integration, which follows what you are doing rather than where you are.

**Will a scheduled block turn itself off automatically?**
It should, and usually does. Jomo maintains a public troubleshooting article specifically because blocks sometimes fail to release on schedule, attributing it to conflicts with other Screen Time apps, Low Power Mode, and outdated software. Running two blockers at once is the most common cause, so pick one.

**Can I bypass my own schedule on iPhone?**
Often, yes. Opal's help center, Freedom's support docs, and AppBlock's developer all confirm a user can disable or weaken their app through Settings, Screen Time. Setting a Screen Time passcode that someone else holds closes most of that gap.

## Final Thoughts

For most people asking this question, Jomo is the right answer on an iPhone. It gives you three trigger types, category groups, and a multi-day lock, for a fraction of what Opal charges, and the company is unusually candid about where its scheduling breaks.

AppBlock is the more interesting pick if your triggers are really about place rather than time, though its iOS version is the weaker half of that product and you should know that going in.

The schedule is the easy part. The hard part is the ten minutes after the block starts, when you are holding a phone that suddenly has nothing on it. If you have no answer for that stretch, no scheduler on this list will hold for long.
