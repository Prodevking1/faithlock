## Key Takeaways

- **Strongest technical block on a phone:** Canopy, because it filters at the network level and blocks competing VPNs. It is also the one users complain about most for overblocking and slowdown.
- **Strongest partner reporting:** Covenant Eyes, whose coverage is genuinely narrower on iPhone than on other platforms.
- **Blocks nothing on purpose:** Accountable2You and Truple's accountability product. That is the design, not a shortcoming, and for some people it is the right design.
- **Free and already installed:** the iOS and Android built-in filters, which no app on this list replaces so much as supplements.
- None of the porn blocker apps below has a permanent free tier. Every one is subscription only.

## How We Scored These Porn Blocker Apps

Publishing the method matters more than the ranking, because it lets you disagree with us usefully. Four criteria, weighted equally:

1. **Coverage.** Does it reach apps and every browser, or only one browser? A filter that covers Safari and nothing else is solving a fraction of the problem.
2. **Tamper resistance.** How many steps to switch it off, and does switching it off notify anyone?
3. **Platform honesty.** Apple restricts what third party apps may do. Products that work around this with accessories or companion apps are marked down, not because the workaround is dishonest but because coverage genuinely suffers.
4. **What it costs to find out.** Free tier, trial length, refund terms.

**What we did not score:** effectiveness at changing behavior. No product on this list has published trial evidence for that, and we are not going to invent a number for it.

**Sourcing rule.** Every weakness below links to a real user review or vendor documentation, checked 2026-08-16. Pricing is described qualitatively because these plans change and a stale figure is worse than no figure.

## 1. Canopy, Best Technical Coverage

**Platforms:** iOS, Android, Windows, macOS, ChromeOS. **Cost:** subscription only, tiers by device count, one week free trial then a 30-day money-back guarantee.

Canopy runs a VPN and network extension on iOS and inspects traffic in real time, and its distinguishing move is blurring individual images rather than blocking a whole site. It also blocks third party VPNs, which closes the most common bypass. Of everything here, it is the closest to a genuine technical block.

**Where it struggles:** roughly 3.8 out of 5 across about 1.3K App Store reviews, with recurring complaints about overblocking ordinary content, battery and data use, and breaking banking or streaming apps ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/canopy-ai-online-safety-app/id1492266682)). On Trustpilot it sits far lower, around 2.2 to 2.5, where the dominant complaint is a VPN connection slow enough to make the phone difficult to use, and refunds not being honored ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/canopy.us)). One reviewer reports finding a way around the filter after a few weeks via image search.

Full breakdown: [Canopy porn blocker review](/resources/canopy-porn-blocker-review).

## 2. Covenant Eyes, Best Partner Reporting

**Platforms:** iOS, Android, Windows, macOS. **Cost:** subscription only, no permanent free tier, 30-day trial with money-back guarantee, monthly, annual and lifetime tiers each covering up to 10 users and unlimited household devices.

The premise is not blocking. On Android, Windows and macOS it takes regular screenshots, blurs and analyzes them with AI to flag explicit content, and sends a report to a partner you choose. On iOS it adds a filtering VPN that blocks adult content at the network level across the device, with the screenshot capture limited to Safari.

**Where it struggles:** that Safari limitation is the important one, because other browsers and apps fall outside the reporting even though the VPN still filters ([TechLockdown](https://www.techlockdown.com/articles/covenant-eyes-iphone-review)). An independent tester installed a dating app and received suggestive images by message without triggering an alert ([Cranky Old Dan](https://crankyolddan.substack.com/p/you-cant-hide-those-covenant-eyes)). Trustpilot reviews report difficulty cancelling and continued billing afterward ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/covenanteyes.com)).

## 3. Accountable2You, Most Honest About Blocking Nothing

**Platforms:** iOS, Android, Windows, macOS, Chromebook, Linux. **Cost:** subscription only, 10-day free trial, Personal and Family tiers.

The company states plainly that this is not a blocking tool. It logs browsing including private browsing, app usage and device activity, then sends real time alerts or daily and weekly summaries to as many partners as you nominate. Unlimited partners per device is unusual and genuinely useful for anyone whose plan involves more than one person.

**Where it struggles:** App Store reviewers describe frequent false alerts, including ordinary beach photos flagged as suspicious, and ask for monitoring to be harder to switch off ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/accountable2you-monitoring/id1531309290)). Coverage of messaging apps is reported as poor. One Chrome Web Store reviewer reports reinstalling the extension daily without resolution ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/accountable2you/efbfdmcbmjkdkhopodcpmajcdbkiljaa/reviews)).

## 4. Truple, Best Encryption Story, Worst iPhone Story

**Platforms:** Android, Windows, macOS, ChromeOS, Linux, Chrome extension. On iOS and iPadOS, through separate companion apps rather than a Truple branded app. **Cost:** two separate subscription products, a cheaper DNS filter and a pricier screenshot accountability app, 30-day money-back guarantee.

Truple is really two things. The DNS filter blocks over two million adult sites, installed on iOS via a configuration profile. The accountability app blocks nothing: it captures random screenshots of the whole device, analyzes them, and shares them with your chosen partner. End-to-end encryption is claimed as a differentiator in this market, which given the sensitivity of screenshot data is a substantive claim rather than a marketing one.

**Where it struggles:** there is no native Truple app on iOS, requiring separate companion apps ([Truple support](https://support.truple.io/articles/ios/iphone-setup-guide)), and the company acknowledges its iOS solutions are workarounds for Apple's limitations with weaker coverage than Android ([Truple support](https://support.truple.io/articles/ios/why-iphone-app-is-designed-the-way-it-is)). The DNS filter is bypassable by changing DNS settings unless the device is supervised.

## 5. Bark, Best for a Parent Who Wants Alerts Rather Than Walls

**Platforms:** iOS, Android, plus the Bark Sync hardware accessory and the Bark Home router device. **Cost:** subscription only, Bark Premium and entry-level Bark Jr, one subscription covering all children and devices in the household.

Bark is a monitoring product first. Its AI scans messages, social media and searches and alerts a parent to risky content, including bullying and suicidal ideation alongside explicit material. It also does category based site and app blocking and screen time management.

**Where it struggles:** on iOS, Apple prevents third party apps from reading messages and photos directly, so Bark sells a physical charger, the Bark Sync, that scans the device when plugged in. Monitoring is therefore periodic rather than continuous ([Bark support](https://support.bark.us/en/articles/13928365-how-to-monitor-ios-devices-with-the-bark-sync)). App Store reviewers report the app being difficult to uninstall without contacting support ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bark-parental-controls/id1477619146)). Bark acknowledges false positives in its alerts.

## 6. Your Phone's Built-In Filter, Best Value in the Category

**Platforms:** iOS and Android. **Cost:** free, already installed.

Not an app, and that is the point. On iPhone, Screen Time's Content & Privacy Restrictions include a Web Content setting with Limit Adult Websites. On Android, Family Link filters Chrome with "Try to block explicit sites" ([Google For Families Help](https://support.google.com/families/answer/7087030)). Paired with a free filtering DNS resolver, this reaches further than most people assume before paying for anything.

**Where it struggles:** it protects exactly as well as the passcode is protected, and Google says the quiet part out loud: "No filter is perfect, but this should help hide sexually explicit sites."

## What About FaithLock

Included here only to rule it out, since readers arriving from a search for porn blocker apps deserve a direct answer: **FaithLock does not belong on this list.** It is not a content filter and it does not report to an accountability partner.

[FaithLock](/) is an iOS app that shields apps you choose using Apple's Family Controls API, and unlocking one requires reading a Bible verse and answering a question about it. It is aimed at habitual reaching rather than at filtering what a browser can load, so it sits alongside the tools above rather than replacing any of them, and it is iOS only.

## When the Answer Is Not on This List

Every product here changes what is easy to reach. None of them treats what may sit underneath, and none is therapy. If the pattern has survived several honest attempts and is affecting sleep, work, or a relationship, the useful next step is a clinician rather than a fourth subscription. The [Psychology Today therapist directory](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/therapists) filters by concern and location, and many therapists list sliding scale fees.

If any of this is bound up with thoughts of self-harm, hopelessness, or a crisis of any kind, that comes first. In the US, the [988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline](https://988lifeline.org/) is free, 24/7, by call, text, or chat, in English and Spanish.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are the best porn blocker apps for iPhone?**
Canopy has the widest technical coverage on iOS because its filtering runs at the network level. Covenant Eyes filters device wide too, though its reporting narrows to Safari. Before either, turn on the built-in Screen Time restriction, which is free and covers web content system wide. Details in [porn blocker for iPhone](/resources/porn-blocker-iphone).

**Are there free porn blocker apps?**
Not among the products on this list, which are all subscription only. Free options exist but they are operating system settings and public DNS resolvers rather than apps. See [free porn blocker](/resources/free-porn-blocker).

**Which porn blocker apps cannot be uninstalled?**
None is genuinely uninstall-proof on a personal device. Some raise the cost: Truple uses device admin locking on Android, and Bark reviewers report needing to contact support to remove it. On an unsupervised iPhone, deletion is always available to whoever holds the passcode.

**What is the difference between a porn blocker app and accountability software?**
A blocker prevents access. Accountability software permits access and tells someone. Several products sell both, which is why the category is confusing. [Porn blocker vs accountability](/resources/porn-blocker-vs-accountability) separates them.

**Do porn blocker apps work on Android?**
Yes, and generally better than on iOS, because Android permits deeper system access. Truple and Covenant Eyes both take screenshots natively on Android where iOS requires workarounds. See [porn blocker for Android](/resources/porn-blocker-android).
