## Quick Verdict

Canopy is the closest thing in this category to a real technical block on a phone, and it is the product to consider when a passcode based restriction is not enough. It filters traffic through a network extension, classifies images in real time rather than blocking whole sites, and blocks third party VPNs so the standard bypass does not work.

The cost of that strength shows up in its own review pages: overblocking of ordinary content, meaningful battery and data use, and enough slowdown that some users describe the phone as difficult to use.

**Editorial score: 3.5 out of 5.** Strong mechanism, real friction. This is our assessment of fit for adult content filtering, not an aggregate of user ratings, which are given separately below.

## Is Canopy Free?

No. Canopy is subscription only with no permanent free tier. It offers a one week free trial, followed by a 30-day money-back guarantee, with Essential, Plus and Premium tiers priced by the number of devices, billed annually on the plans observed (verified on the vendor's pricing page, checked 2026-08-16).

Two things to know before you start the trial. First, several Trustpilot reviewers report that the 30-day refund was not honored in their case, so treat the guarantee as a stated policy rather than a certainty ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/canopy.us), checked 2026-08-16). Second, because pricing is by device count, a household with several phones lands in a higher tier than the headline plan suggests. Check the device math before the trial ends.

If a free option is what you need, the built-in filters on iOS and Android plus a free DNS resolver cover the same web content, without reporting or image classification. Those are listed in [free porn blocker](/resources/free-porn-blocker).

## How Canopy Works

Canopy is owned by Netspark Inc., whose founder and CEO is Sean Clifford (as reported by [Focus on the Family's Daily Citizen](https://dailycitizen.focusonthefamily.com/canopy-new-parental-control-app-protects-children-from-nudity-and-pornography/), checked 2026-08-16). It runs on iOS, Android, Windows, macOS and ChromeOS.

The mechanism has three parts:

**A network extension on the device.** On iOS this installs as a VPN profile, visible afterward under Settings, VPN & Device Management ([Canopy support](https://support.canopy.us/portal/en/kb/articles/canopy-shield-ios-installation), checked 2026-08-16). Because it sits on traffic rather than inside a browser, it reaches apps as well as every browser on the device. That is the single most important fact about Canopy, and it is what separates it from extension based tools.

**Real time image classification.** Rather than blocking an entire site because part of it is objectionable, Canopy inspects traffic and blurs specific images. In principle this means a news site or a social platform stays usable while individual images do not load, which is a more precise instrument than domain level blocking.

**VPN blocking.** Canopy blocks third party VPNs. This matters more than it sounds, because installing another VPN is the standard way around a filtering profile. Closing it is one of the few genuine anti-bypass measures in this market.

It also ships category filtering across 20 or more web and app categories, AI tool category blocking including ChatGPT enabled by default, and sextortion attempt detection.

## What Its Users Actually Report

Canopy holds roughly 3.8 out of 5 across about 1.3K App Store reviews, and around 2.2 to 2.5 on Trustpilot. That gap is itself informative: App Store reviews skew toward people who got it working, Trustpilot toward people who had a billing or support experience.

Three complaints recur often enough to treat as characteristics rather than incidents:

**Overblocking.** Non-explicit content blocked, and incompatibilities with banking or streaming apps ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/canopy-ai-online-safety-app/id1492266682), checked 2026-08-16). This is the predictable cost of real time classification. A filter making judgment calls thousands of times a day will make some of them wrong, and the direction of the error is set by the vendor's tolerance, not yours.

**Speed and battery.** Trustpilot reviewers describe the VPN connection as slow enough to make the iPhone nearly unusable, alongside heavy battery and data consumption ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/canopy.us), checked 2026-08-16). Routing all traffic through an inspection layer has a cost, and on older hardware that cost is visible.

**It is not airtight.** One App Store reviewer reports finding ways around the filter after a few weeks, notably through image search ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/canopy-ai-online-safety-app/id1492266682), checked 2026-08-16). Worth holding onto if you are buying Canopy expecting a solved problem rather than a raised cost.

## Where Canopy Is the Right Choice

- A child's or teenager's device, where you need coverage across apps and the person may look for a way around it. The VPN blocking is the reason to pick Canopy specifically.
- Any situation where nobody can hold a Screen Time passcode, so the defense has to live in software rather than in a person.
- Households wanting one product across mixed platforms, since it runs on all five major ones.

## Where It Is Not

- If you want a report sent to an accountability partner, Canopy is not built for that. Covenant Eyes or Accountable2You are, and the difference is explained in [porn blocker vs accountability](/resources/porn-blocker-vs-accountability).
- If your device is older, or you depend on banking and streaming apps working without argument, the reported friction is likely to be your experience too.
- If you have not yet turned on the free built-in filter, start there. It costs nothing and rules out the possibility that you are buying a solution to a problem the phone already solves.

## Alternatives Worth Comparing

**Covenant Eyes.** Also filters device wide on iOS through a VPN, and adds a report to a partner you choose. Its screenshot capture on iOS covers Safari only, leaving other browsers and apps outside the report ([TechLockdown](https://www.techlockdown.com/articles/covenant-eyes-iphone-review), checked 2026-08-16). See [Covenant Eyes review](/resources/covenant-eyes-review) and [Canopy vs Covenant Eyes](/resources/canopy-vs-covenant-eyes).

**Bark.** A monitoring product rather than a filter, alerting a parent to risky content across messages and social platforms. On iOS its coverage is periodic because it depends on a physical accessory, the Bark Sync ([Bark support](https://support.bark.us/en/articles/13928365-how-to-monitor-ios-devices-with-the-bark-sync), checked 2026-08-16).

**The free stack.** Built-in OS restriction plus filtering DNS. No image classification, no reporting, no cost. For many adults filtering their own device, this is genuinely sufficient.

**FaithLock.** Included only to rule it out, since some readers arrive here comparing anything that limits a phone. [FaithLock](/) is an iOS app that shields apps you choose using Apple's Family Controls API, requiring a Bible verse quiz to reopen one. It is not a content filter, it does not classify images, and it does not report to anyone. Different problem, different tool.

## The Verdict

If I needed adult content filtering on a device where the person using it might look for a way around it, I would pick Canopy, because the combination of network level filtering and VPN blocking is not available elsewhere at this price. I would go in expecting to spend the first fortnight adding exceptions for legitimate sites and apps, and I would not choose it for an older phone.

For an adult filtering their own device with a willing person to hold the passcode, I would not buy Canopy at all. The free built-in restriction plus a filtering DNS resolver does the same job, and the difference in outcome comes from the passcode holder rather than from the software.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does Canopy cost?**
Canopy is subscription only, with Essential, Plus and Premium tiers priced by device count and billed annually on the plans observed, after a one week free trial (verified on the vendor's pricing page, checked 2026-08-16). Because plans scale with devices, price a multi-phone household before the trial ends rather than after.

**Does Canopy have a free trial?**
Yes, one week, followed by a 30-day money-back guarantee. Several Trustpilot reviewers report the refund not being honored in their case, so cancel deliberately rather than relying on the guarantee.

**Can Canopy be bypassed?**
It is harder to bypass than most, because it blocks third party VPNs. It is not impossible: one App Store reviewer describes finding a route around the filter after a few weeks via image search. On an unsupervised device, anyone able to remove the configuration profile can remove the filter.

**Does Canopy slow down your phone?**
Some users say significantly. Routing all traffic through an inspection layer has a real cost, and Trustpilot reviews include reports of the connection being slow enough to make the phone difficult to use. Use the trial specifically to test this on your own hardware.

**Is Canopy better than Covenant Eyes?**
They answer different questions. Canopy is the better filter, particularly for coverage across apps. Covenant Eyes is the better accountability tool, because its model is a report to a person you choose. If you want both, Covenant Eyes filters device wide on iOS as well, with reporting narrower than its filtering.
