## One Sec Overview

One Sec, created by Frederik Riedel in Barcelona, is a screen time and focus app that has earned an impressive 4.8-star rating from over 55,000 users. Its core mechanic—a simple 10-second pause before opening distracting apps—is grounded in behavioral psychology research. A peer-reviewed study conducted with the Max Planck Institute demonstrated that One Sec users experienced a 57% reduction in app usage, lending scientific credibility to its friction-based approach.

The app's strength lies in its cross-platform availability. Unlike FaithLock, One Sec works seamlessly on iOS, Android, macOS, Windows, and Linux, making it ideal for users who switch between devices or work across multiple ecosystems. Its features include scheduled app blocking, website blocking during focus periods, a "Good Morning Countdown" that prevents app access for the first 15 minutes after waking, and comprehensive usage tracking through an intuitive dashboard.

For power users, One Sec integrates with iOS Shortcuts, enabling sophisticated automation workflows. The pricing structure—free tier, $19 annual Pro subscription, or $99.99 lifetime—offers flexibility. The free version provides essential features, while Pro unlocks advanced scheduling, website blocking, and priority support.

One Sec's secular, science-based philosophy appeals to anyone seeking evidence-backed digital wellness without religious language. It's particularly popular among productivity-focused professionals, developers, and minimalists. However, it lacks any spiritual or faith dimension, which may feel impersonal to Christians seeking deeper meaning in their digital discipline.

## Feature-by-Feature Comparison

### App Blocking Approach

**One Sec** uses a behavioral friction model: when you tap a blocked app, a 10-second countdown appears with a "pause and breathe" prompt. After the timer expires, the app opens. This delay is short enough to feel non-punitive but long enough to interrupt the automatic habit loop. Research shows this micro-friction works by creating a moment of conscious choice.

**FaithLock** employs a spiritual friction model: tapping a blocked app triggers a Bible verse (rotated from a curated library). Users must read and reflect on the verse before unlocking. This takes approximately 30 seconds—three times longer than One Sec. The trade-off is psychological: instead of mere delay, users encounter Scripture. The unlock becomes an intentional spiritual practice.

**Winner for convenience:** One Sec (faster, less friction). **Winner for spiritual growth:** FaithLock (Bible exposure, intentionality).

### Faith vs. Science

**One Sec** is built on peer-reviewed behavioral science. The Max Planck Institute study provides credible evidence for its effectiveness. The approach is universally applicable—anyone, regardless of belief system, can benefit from the pause mechanism. This universality is One Sec's strength and appeals to secular audiences, agnostics, and those uncomfortable with explicit faith messaging.

**FaithLock** is grounded in Christian theology: the belief that Scripture is living and active (Hebrews 4:12), that temptation is overcome through spiritual practices, and that discipline originates from the Spirit (2 Timothy 1:7). While not peer-reviewed in academic journals, FaithLock's framework is validated by centuries of Christian spiritual practice and contemporary testimonies from its community. For believers, Bible verses carry inherent power that no secular timer can replicate.

**Winner for universal accessibility:** One Sec. **Winner for Christian integration:** FaithLock.

### Usage Tracking & Streaks

**One Sec** offers sophisticated usage dashboards showing daily/weekly/monthly app usage trends, helping users identify problem apps and understand their patterns objectively. The data visualization is beautiful and motivating.

**FaithLock** combines usage insights with streak tracking via its 30-day "covenant" system. Users can commit publicly to a digital fast, view their streak of consecutive covenant days, and track how many Bible verses they've encountered. This gamification appeals to habit-formation psychology while adding spiritual accountability. Streaks can be shared within a community (if FaithLock launches a community feature).

**Winner for detailed analytics:** One Sec. **Winner for motivational streaks:** FaithLock.

### Platform Availability

**One Sec** is available on iOS, Android, and desktop browsers (Windows, Mac, Linux). A user can install One Sec across all their devices and maintain consistent blocking rules. This cross-platform functionality is particularly valuable for people who use multiple operating systems or share devices.

**FaithLock** is currently iOS-only, with Android expected in Q2 2026. This is a significant limitation for Android users or those needing multi-device support. However, for the iOS-dominant segments (Apple users, iPhone-first consumers), this limitation is irrelevant.

**Winner:** One Sec (by far). Cross-platform is a massive advantage for digital wellness because phone blocking loses impact if users simply switch to a laptop or different phone.
