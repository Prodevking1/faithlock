## 3. Opal Overview

Opal is a meticulously engineered screen time control app that treats digital addiction like a game you can win. With over 4 million active users, Opal has validated that people respond powerfully to visible progress metrics, achievement systems, and comparative scoring. Opal's claim of saving users 120+ million hours from distraction is staggering—if accurate, it suggests profound engagement and effectiveness across its massive user base.

Opal's core innovation is the focus score: a calculated metric showing your improvement in reducing distracting app usage. Unlike simple "hours saved" tracking, the focus score reflects meaningful behavioral change. You watch the number climb daily, creating visible confirmation that your efforts work. This is powerful psychology—humans are inherently motivated by measurable progress.

The rewards system is sophisticated. As you reduce screen time and maintain focus streaks, you unlock rewards: badges, visual achievements, special in-app recognition. You can compare your focus score with friends, creating friendly competition that drives adherence. For competitive users, Opal's system is psychologically addictive in the right way—you're addicted to improving, not to distraction.

Opal's deep focus mode goes beyond simple app blocking. It detects when you're likely to need focus (working hours, study time) and proactively enables protections. The app learns your patterns and anticipates your needs. This is significantly more sophisticated than manual blocking, creating an app blocker that thinks alongside you.

However, Opal is expensive: $100 annually for premium features. The free version exists but with limited functionality. Additionally, Opal offers zero faith-based components. If you're Christian and believe your battle with phone addiction is spiritual, Opal won't recognize that dimension of your struggle. It treats digital wellness as pure productivity optimization, not spiritual discipline.
