## 3. Cold Turkey Overview

Cold Turkey is legendarily extreme. It's not an app blocker in the conventional sense—it's a digital cage. When activated, Cold Turkey's Frozen Turkey mode locks your entire operating system until the timer expires. You cannot restart your device, cannot boot into safe mode, cannot force-quit the application. Your computer is literally frozen, and the only way out is to wait or physically restart the machine (which wipes Frozen Turkey's lock, but the metaphorical damage is done—you just destroyed your system to disable an app blocker).

This is intentional design philosophy. Cold Turkey's creator believes that app blockers fail because users circumvent them. Every bypass mechanism—disabling the app, uninstalling it, restarting—represents a failure point. Cold Turkey eliminates failure points by making the computer itself inaccessible. You haven't disabled the blocker; the blocker has literally disabled your computer.

Frozen Turkey is available in scheduled and unscheduled modes. Unscheduled Frozen Turkey (launched manually) locks your computer for a set duration—30 minutes, 2 hours, whatever you specify. Scheduled Frozen Turkey runs at designated times (9am-5pm weekdays, for example), automatically locking your computer during those windows. You don't bypass a scheduled lock; you just wait until the window ends.

Cold Turkey's website blocking is sophisticated. It filters both HTTPS and HTTP traffic, blocks sites at the DNS level, and even interferes with VPN workarounds. The philosophy is: if someone can technically bypass it, Cold Turkey finds a way to block that bypass too.

One-time $39 pricing is genuinely attractive. Unlike subscription-based blockers, Cold Turkey charges once and works forever (with optional updates). This is a powerful value proposition for users willing to pay upfront to solve desktop distraction permanently.

However, Cold Turkey has critical limitations. First, it's desktop-only. Zero mobile support. If your distraction problem is primarily smartphone usage (the reality for most people), Cold Turkey is irrelevant. Second, Frozen Turkey mode is so extreme that many users find it oppressive. Some users appreciate the forcefulness; others perceive it as Draconian. Third, the one-time payment means no ongoing development incentives; newer operating system compatibility issues eventually emerge.

## 4. FaithLock Overview

FaithLock is the opposite of Cold Turkey in almost every dimension. Rather than extreme technical control, FaithLock uses gentle spiritual influence. Rather than locking your entire device, FaithLock makes individual apps retrievable only through reading Scripture. Rather than permanent one-time payment, FaithLock uses affordable annual subscription.

FaithLock's core insight is that for Christians, the most powerful accountability mechanism isn't a locked computer—it's spiritual conviction. When you reach for Instagram, FaithLock doesn't freeze your phone. Instead, it presents you with Scripture: "For the flesh desires what is contrary to the Spirit, and the Spirit what is contrary to the flesh" (Galatians 5:17). You read that verse, reflect on it, and the psychological work—the confrontation between impulse and conviction—prevents access as effectively as Cold Turkey's technical lock.

FaithLock's 30-day covenant system adds behavioral structure. You're not just blocking apps indefinitely; you're committing to 30-day cycles of accountability, completing covenants, and starting fresh with renewed intention. Prayer reminders throughout the day connect your digital wellness struggle to your spiritual foundation.

The pricing is accessible: $24.99 annually for premium features. This democratizes faith-based digital accountability for users who might never pay Cold Turkey's $39 one-time fee or manage ongoing subscriptions.

However, FaithLock is iOS-only. It doesn't address desktop distraction. If your problem is checking email compulsively on a Windows desktop or doom-scrolling Reddit on a Mac, FaithLock is completely irrelevant. Additionally, FaithLock's psychological barriers are weaker than Cold Turkey's technical barriers for users not motivated by faith or who lack self-awareness of their covenant commitment.
